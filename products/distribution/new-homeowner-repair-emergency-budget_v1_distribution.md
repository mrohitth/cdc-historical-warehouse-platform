**[ADVICE] The 1% rule for home repairs — and why your first year will cost more**

Bought our house 14 months ago. Had $0 in savings for repairs because all our cash went to the down payment and closing costs. Within 8 months we'd had: $3,200 HVAC compressor failure, $800 plumber for a pipe that froze, $1,100 refrigerator replacement.

I learned the hard way what I should have known upfront.

**The 1% rule:** Save 1–2% of your home's value annually for repairs and maintenance. On a $400k house, that's $333–$667/month. I wasn't saving anything. That was a mistake.

**Year one is more expensive.** Your first year will likely run 1.5–2% because the previous owner's deferred maintenance surfaces immediately. Budget for this.

**The triage framework saved me from panic calls:**

- True emergency (gas smell, flooding, no heat in winter): call immediately, pay emergency rates
- Urgent but not emergency (no hot water, active leak): schedule within 1 week
- Can wait (cosmetic, non-structural): 30–90 days

Learning to classify before calling saved me from paying $250 emergency fees for a $20 DIY fix.

**What I wish I'd done on day one:** Opened a separate savings account, set up $200/month automatic transfer, and printed a triage checklist.
