**Post Body:**

---

This is a follow-up to a post I made a few months ago where I admitted I had no idea what my kid was actually learning because everything was on a school Chromebook I couldn't see into.

I got a lot of responses from parents in the same situation. I also got some useful advice. Here is what actually worked.

**The problem, as I understood it:**
My kid's school went 1:1 Chromebook three years ago. Graded papers stopped coming home. The backpack folder that used to have returned work disappeared. When I asked my kid what grades they had, they shrugged. When I asked the teacher, I got directed to a parent portal I didn't know existed and couldn't figure out how to use.

I felt like I was flying blind on the most important job I have.

**What I did:**

1. **Got portal access.** Took me 15 minutes. Searched "[my district] parent portal login," found it was PowerSchool, created an account using the activation email from school. Why this isn't communicated to every parent at the start of the year is a separate rant.

2. **Set up notifications.** I get an email now when a grade drops or an assignment goes missing. I do not have to remember to check — the information comes to me.

3. **Sunday night portal check.** Every Sunday I spend 5 minutes looking at what's due, what's missing, what grades came in that week. If something is off, we address it Monday morning.

4. **The five questions.** I ask my kid every school night: What was interesting? What was hard? Anything due this week? Anything you need help with? I do not get real answers every time. But over weeks, patterns emerge.

5. **Monthly teacher email.** I send a brief "checking in, anything I should know?" email to each teacher once a month. This sounds small but it works. Teachers are more likely to flag issues proactively when they know you're paying attention.

**The result:**
I actually know what my kid's grades are now. When something starts slipping, I catch it in week 3 instead of week 10. My kid knows I'm paying attention, which means they take homework more seriously.

The Chromebook didn't go away. But I stopped feeling shut out.
