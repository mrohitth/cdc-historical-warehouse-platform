**Title:** "The 'drop the rope' method for potty regression after a new baby — it actually works"
**Post Body:**

---

Potty regression after a new sibling is one of the most common things that happens in early childhood, and somehow there's almost no straight talk about it.

Here's what nobody tells you in the hospital when you're bringing home baby #2: your older kid might completely lose their potty training. It can show up as accidents, refusal, demanding diapers back, withholding, all of it. It's not because you did anything wrong. It's a developmentally normal response to a massive life change.

After going through this with my older kid and talking to pediatricians and child psychologists about it, here's the thing that actually works: **stop engaging**.

Not ignoring your kid — ignoring the potty. No prompts. No reminders. No "do you need to go?" No "remember to tell mommy when you need to potty." No reactions to accidents, even frustrated ones.

It's called the "drop the rope" method, and the reason it works is that most potty regressions in this situation are being reinforced by parental attention — even negative attention. When your kid has an accident and you react with frustration, concern, a long talk, or even a neutral "let's clean this up," you're inadvertently rewarding the behavior. Your kid knows they'll get a reaction, and in a household where the new baby is getting most of the attention, that reaction is valuable currency.

**What to actually do:**

Week 1: Stop all prompts, reminders, and reactions to accidents. Just clean it up neutrally and move on. "You had an accident. Let's clean it up." That's it.

Week 2: Add 10 minutes of completely uninterrupted one-on-one time with your older kid every single day. No phone, no baby, just them. This fills the attention tank that's been drained by the new arrival.

Week 3: Watch for them to start initiating potty on their own. When they do, respond calmly and matter-of-factly. No celebration explosion, no sticker chart. Just a brief acknowledgment and move on.

The whole thing typically resolves in 2–6 weeks with this approach.

**The one thing that prolongs it:** Letting other caregivers (grandparents, nannies, partners) use different approaches. If someone is still bribing or punishing, the regression will hold. Everyone in the household needs to be on the same page.

**When it's medical:** If your kid is withholding, straining, having very large or hard stools, or showing sudden onset regression with pain, see your pediatrician. Chronic constipation and UTIs are frequently missed causes of potty regression that behavioral approaches can't fix.
