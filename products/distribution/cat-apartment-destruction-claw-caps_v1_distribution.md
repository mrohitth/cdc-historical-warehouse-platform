**Post body:**

My cat has very large claws and he was using them to absolutely demolish my apartment. I've been where you are — reading comments at 2am, trying claw caps that fell off in two days, wondering if maybe rehoming was the responsible thing to do.

Here's what I learned: claw caps do work. They fail because most people don't know the sizing rules, the application method, or the replacement schedule. I want to share what actually works so you don't have to figure it out the hard way.

**The claw cap system that actually works:**

First: size matters more than anything. Caps come in small through extra-large. Most people use medium when they need large, or large when they need XL. If the cap falls off within a day, go up a size — not because the cat is too big, but because the claw is too wide for that cap.

Second: clean claws matter. Adhesive bonds to keratin, not to the oil and gunk that accumulates on claws. Wipe each claw with a damp cotton ball before applying. Apply in a dry room with low humidity.

Third: the replacement schedule is non-negotiable. Caps come off when the outer claw sheath sheds — every 4-6 weeks. If you miss a cycle, your cat has exposed claws again. Set a calendar reminder. This is the step most people skip.

**For renters specifically:**

If you live in an apartment and your cat is scratching door frames and walls — clear plastic corner guards are your best friend. They peel off at move-out with no damage. Cost me $15 and saved my security deposit.

For walls: clear adhesive film in the scratching zone. Cats hate the texture. It buys you time while you work on the behavioral side.

**The underlying issue:**

Most apartment scratching is displacement behavior — your cat doesn't have enough vertical territory, enough hunting simulation, or enough environmental complexity. Two 7-minute interactive play sessions per day (wand toy, you moving the toy like prey) reduces destructive scratching more than any physical barrier.
