I want to share what actually worked for my cat's early morning meowing problem — the kind that makes you genuinely question whether you can keep doing this.

For months, my cat would start yowling around 4:45 AM and wouldn't stop until I gave in and fed her. I tried ignoring it. I tried clicking my tongue, pushing her off the bed, telling her no. Nothing worked for more than a few days before she figured it out and went back to the same routine.

What I learned: cats aren't broken. Their behavior is just very precisely optimized for what works. Every time I responded to her meowing — even negatively — I was teaching her that meowing eventually pays off.

The fix wasn't complicated but it required following a specific protocol:

**1. Cut the feedback entirely for 14 days.** Any response (including yelling) reinforces the behavior. I mean nothing — no eye contact, no speech, no movement. The first 3 days were genuinely brutal. She escalated hard.

**2. Installed an automatic feeder.** This was the key. The feeder dispenses at 6:00 AM regardless of what she does. Now she associates the sound with food — not her meowing. The timing is 30 minutes later than her "expected" wake time, and after two weeks I pushed it back by 15 minutes every few days.

**3. Play session before bed.** 15 minutes of high-intensity wand toy play around 9 PM. This drains the energy that would otherwise hit at 4 AM.

The whole thing took about 5 weeks to fully consolidate. Now she waits by the feeder in the morning instead of screaming at my face.

If you're in the thick of this — you're not broken, your cat isn't "bad," and there's a structural fix.

I made a free checklist that walks through the protocol step by step. Happy to share if it would help.
