**Title:** What nobody tells you about the 4-month sleep regression: it's not actually a regression

---

**Post body:**

My daughter turned four months old three weeks ago and within 48 hours, our sleep situation went from "finally getting 4-hour stretches" to "waking every 45 minutes all night."

I spent two days thinking I had broken something. I had not changed anything. She was fine, then she was not fine, and I could not figure out what I had done wrong.

Here is what I learned that nobody told me upfront:

**The 4-month regression is not a regression. It is a neurological milestone.**

Around month four, your baby's brain reorganizes sleep from the primitive newborn pattern into mature REM/NREM cycles — the same architecture adults have. This is your baby's brain doing exactly what it is supposed to do.

The problem: your baby now has to learn to navigate these new sleep stages. Newborns could sleep through the transitions between cycles. Four-month-olds wake up more fully at those same transitions and need to either self-soothe back or signal for help.

This is why the waking feels so sudden. Baby is not waking more — baby is waking more completely.

**What helped us get through it:**

- Wake windows of 75–120 minutes. We were pushing to 90–100 minutes and it was making things worse. Dropping to 75–85 minutes immediately improved nap quality.

- Dark room, white noise, cool temperature. The nap environment that worked during the newborn phase was no longer sufficient. We blackouted the room fully and that alone extended naps by 20–30 minutes.

- Minimal overnight interaction. When she wakes at night, we keep lights off, voices below a whisper, and we wait 10–15 minutes before picking her up. Some of those wake-ups resolve on their own.

- Shift system with my partner. We split nights so each of us gets at least one unbroken 4-hour block. Neither of us is fully rested, but neither of us is collapsing.

**What nobody told me that I will tell you now:**

This lasts 2–6 weeks. It is finite. You did not cause it. You cannot prevent it. The phase where your baby learns to connect sleep cycles will end, and most babies come out of it sleeping better than before.

I put together a free quick-reference checklist covering wake windows, the overnight protocol, and the red-flag symptoms that mean you should call your pediatrician. It is formatted to print and keep by the crib. I would have found something like this useful at 2am when I could not remember what a reasonable wake window was.

No pitch. No follow-up emails. Just useful information that might make the next few weeks slightly less brutal.

Happy to answer questions from parents going through this right now. We are on the other side of it now and it does get better.
