**Post body:**

Eight months into homeownership and I was having nightmares about what would break next. Drain leak. Slab leak. Full plumbing project. I was dipping into reserves and feeling like there was no end in sight.

I want to share what I learned so you don't have to figure this out the way I did.

**The number nobody gave you:**

The average American homeowner spends $21,084 per year on non-mortgage home expenses. Property taxes, insurance, utilities, maintenance, repairs, landscaping. This is Bankrate data (2025) and Hippo Insurance data (2026) — not anecdotal.

Forty-two percent of homeowners say these costs were more expensive than expected. You are not alone.

**The maintenance timeline nobody explains:**

- Year 1: Deferred maintenance reveal. Your inspector missed things. They always do.
- Years 2-3: Appliance failures. Water heaters, dishwashers, garbage disposals. They fail on a schedule.
- Years 3-5: Systems aging. Roof, HVAC, plumbing. The expensive stuff.

The industry standard is 1-2% of home value per year for maintenance. A $400K home: $4,000-$8,000 annually, not counting taxes and insurance.

**What actually helped:**

The $1/month per $1,000 home value emergency fund formula. A $400K home = $400/month in a dedicated savings account for home repairs. Automated. Separate from your regular savings.

It doesn't cover everything. But knowing the buffer exists changes the emotional relationship you have with your home.

I put together a complete guide covering all of this — the full cost breakdown, the 5-year maintenance calendar, the emergency triage framework (what actually needs a professional today vs. what can wait), and the anxiety management framework. Also includes a free printable maintenance checklist.
