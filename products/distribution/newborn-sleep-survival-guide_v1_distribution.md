Look, I've been where you are. That 3am ceiling stare. The math you've done on cumulative sleep debt that would make an accountant weep. The part where someone tells you to "sleep when the baby sleeps" and you want to throw something.

I'm not going to tell you it gets better. You know it does. What you need is a system for right now.

Here's what actually moved the needle for us:

**The shift system.** Not "we'll figure it out." Not "take shifts randomly." A real schedule. One parent is completely off-duty for a 4-hour block every night. Earbuds in. Door closed. The other parent handles everything. Then you switch. Four hours minimum. That's the threshold for your brain to actually recover. Anything less is just fragmented unconsciousness.

**White noise at the right volume.** 70 decibels. Not 40. Not whatever your phone goes to. 70dB is equivalent to a vacuum cleaner in the next room. It suppresses the startle reflex that wakes babies up between sleep cycles. This alone extended our longest stretch by 90 minutes.

**Wake windows.** Newborns can only stay awake 45-60 minutes before overtiredness kicks in. Overtiredness causes cortisol spikes that make falling asleep harder, which creates more overtiredness. It's a loop. Once we started watching for sleepy cues and putting the baby down before the second wind hit, the crying-at-bedtime stopped.

**The 12-week timeline.** Your baby is not defective. Newborns have no circadian rhythm until around 6 weeks, and melatonin production starts around then too. Before 4 months, their sleep architecture is fundamentally immature. You are not failing. You are waiting for a developmental process.

The stuff I wish someone had told me at week 2:

- The 4-month sleep regression is a permanent neurological change, not a phase you'll cause by doing something wrong
- "Fuss it out" is not the same as CIO — there's a middle ground where you're present and listening but not intervning on every partial waking
- The witching hour (usually 5-9pm) is developmental, not a sign you're doing something wrong, and it peaks around weeks 6-8
