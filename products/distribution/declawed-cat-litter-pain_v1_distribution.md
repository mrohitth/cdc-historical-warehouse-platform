I spent years feeling like a terrible cat owner.

My declawed cat, Luna, started avoiding the litter box about two years after I adopted her. I assumed it was behavioral — maybe she was stressed, maybe she didn't like the litter, maybe she was mad at me for something.

I tried everything. Every litter brand. Every cleaning product. Enzymatic cleaners, enzyme bomb diffusers, enzymatic cleaner you name it. I called the vet. I asked aboutUTIs, crystals, anything medical. All clear.

Nothing worked.

Then I started researching feline pain management, and everything changed.

Here's what I learned that nobody told me: **Declawing isn't just nail removal — it's toe bone amputation.** The last bone of each toe is removed. That changes how weight distributes across the paw. And in many cats, it causes nerve pain that can persist for years.

**The litter box connection:**

If your declawed cat avoids the litter box, consider this: traditional litter isn't soft. Clay litter, silica litter — these can apply uncomfortable pressure or friction to sensitive paw tissue. When a cat learns that using the box hurts, she starts avoiding it. Not out of spite. Out of pain.

Some solutions that help:

- Paper-based litters (Yesterday's News, Carefresh) are consistently softer
- Low-entry boxes remove the barrier that can be hard to step over with sensitive paws
- Nail caps (Soft Paws) provide a smooth surface between paw and litter during retraining
- Pain management options like gabapentin, cold laser therapy exist

I'm not sharing this to shame anyone. I declawed Luna because it was routinely recommended and I didn't know better. But once I understood the pain connection, everything made more sense.

There's a free checklist in my profile that breaks down the accommodation steps if anyone wants something to work from.

For anyone dealing with this — you're not alone, and it's not your fault.
