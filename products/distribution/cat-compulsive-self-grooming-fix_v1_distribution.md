**Title:** What to do when steroids don't work and your cat keeps licking itself raw

**Body:**

My cat has been compulsively licking the same spot on his leg for three months. Bald. Then raw. Then bloody. Vets have given him steroids twice. It clears up for a week and comes back the moment we stop.

Sound familiar?

I spent weeks digging into the research, talking to vets, and trying everything. Here's what's not commonly explained:

**1. "Nothing medical is wrong" often means "we didn't run the right tests."**

In a published veterinary study, 76% of cats presenting with presumed psychogenic alopecia (compulsive grooming with no obvious cause) had an identifiable medical cause once proper diagnostics were done. The most commonly missed: UTIs, hyperthyroidism, food allergies, dental pain, and GI inflammation (IBD).

If your vet did a physical exam and said "it's probably stress" — push for:
- Full urinalysis with culture (not just dipstick)
- Thyroid panel (T4) — especially if your cat is over 7
- 8-12 week food elimination trial (not "a few days on a new food")
- Dental X-rays under sedation (if your cat grooms its face or front paws)
- Abdominal ultrasound (if there's any GI history)

**2. When it IS behavioral, steroids don't work. Gabapentin does.**

Steroids suppress inflammation. They don't address anxiety-driven compulsive behavior. Gabapentin (a feline anxiolytic) has become first-line for anxiety-based grooming in veterinary behavior. Clonazepam is a short-term option. Fluoxetine works for long-term cases but takes 4-6 weeks.

None of these are sedatives or tranquilizers. They reduce the compulsive urge.

**3. The cone is not a solution. Recovery suits are.**

The plastic cone stops the licking but causes stress in most cats. Recovery suits (fabric cat bodysuits) protect the skin while letting your cat move, eat, and sleep normally. They take 1-2 days to adapt to. For most owners, this is a better long-term option.

**4. Enrichment breaks the anxiety loop.**

The compulsive grooming is self-reinforcing — it releases endorphins. The cat is stuck. Breaking the loop requires:
- 2-3 structured play sessions per day (wand toys, hunting games)
- Food puzzles instead of bowls
- Feliway Classic diffuser in the home
- Vertical space (cat trees, shelves)
- Routine — cats are stress out by unpredictability
