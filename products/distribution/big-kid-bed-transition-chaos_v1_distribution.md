**Title:** The "big kid bed disaster" posts — here's the actual framework that prevents them

**Body:**

Every few days someone posts about a big kid bed transition going off the rails. I've seen the same failure modes over and over — not because the parents did anything wrong, but because they went in without a plan for the three things that actually cause the chaos.

Here's what I've found works:

**1. The timing question is signal-based, not age-based.**
Most chaos comes from transitions started for the wrong reason ("new baby in 4 weeks, better get this done"). If you need a rule: either start 8+ weeks before a new sibling arrives, or wait until the new baby is 3 months old. Also: 35" height (AAP guideline) and consistent crib-escape attempts are the two physical readiness signals. Either one alone is enough.

**2. The room has to be fully childproofed before night one — not mostly.**
This is the step most people skip. A crib contains. A bed does not. Anchor all furniture. Gate the bedroom door and any stairs. Window guards. Outlet covers. Cord management. Doorknob covers on any door you need closed. If your toddler can open the bedroom door at 2am, you're supervising all night.

**3. Have a 72-hour plan before you start.**
Whatever response pattern you establish in the first 3 nights becomes the template. Return the child calmly, brief "it's sleep time," tuck in, leave. Check in every 5 minutes with a timer. Don't improvise in the moment — decide the response before bedtime on night one.

**4. The regression is expected and has a timeline.**
Blueberry Pediatrics (pediatrician-reviewed) says regressions from premature or unplanned transitions typically resolve in 6 weeks. Structured response (sticker chart, consistent returns, matter-of-fact scripts) reduces this. If it's still bad at 6 weeks, bring a sleep log to your pediatrician.
