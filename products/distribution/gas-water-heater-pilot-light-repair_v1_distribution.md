**Title:** DIY fix for water heater pilot light that won't stay lit — took me 30 minutes and $13

**Post:**
I'm not a plumber. I've never played one on the internet. But I just fixed my gas water heater that had no hot water for 3 days, and the plumber quote was $247 just to show up.

The fix was a $13 universal thermocouple from Home Depot and about 30 minutes of my time. I'm going to write this up so the next person doesn't spend $200 to learn their water heater just needed a $13 part.

**Background:** Pilot light would ignite, stay lit while I held the button down, and go out immediately when I released it. Classic thermocouple failure.

**What I learned:**
1. The thermocouple is a safety device — it detects whether the pilot is lit and keeps the gas valve open. When it fails, the gas shuts off even if the pilot is burning.
2. The replacement is straightforward: disconnect the old one from the gas valve and pilot assembly, insert the new one (tip must be IN the flame, not just near it), reconnect, relight.
3. The step most people skip: hold the pilot button down for a full 60 seconds after lighting. The thermocouple needs that long to heat up and generate the voltage to hold the valve open.

**What I bought:** Universal thermocouple, $13. Also grabbed a can of compressed air to clean the pilot orifice while I was in there.

**What I didn't buy:** A plumber.

If your pilot goes out immediately when you release the button, try this before calling anyone. The hardest part is getting into the access panel if your water heater is in a tight closet.
