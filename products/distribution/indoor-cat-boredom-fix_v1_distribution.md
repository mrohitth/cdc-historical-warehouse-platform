## Reddit Bridge Post

**Title:** "The bored cat checklist: what actually works vs. what is a waste of money"

**Subreddit:** r/CatAdvice

**Post Body:**

My cat was destroying my apartment. Scratching the sofa, knocking things off every surface, yowling at 4am. I tried everything from stern "no"s to double-sided tape on the furniture.

Nothing worked for more than a few days.

Then I talked to a cat behaviorist (yes, that is a real profession). She explained that destructive cat behavior is almost always an enrichment deficit, not a "bad cat" problem. Cats that are bored create their own entertainment, and you will not like what they choose.

The enrichment hierarchy the behaviorist gave me, ranked by impact:

1. Food puzzles (highest impact — restores the hunting sequence)
2. Vertical space (cat shelving, window perches)
3. Daily interactive play (15 minutes before dinner)
4. Environmental rotation (rotate toys weekly)

I started with just a $15 food puzzle. Within 2 weeks, the nighttime yowling stopped. Within a month, the scratching was significantly reduced.

The food puzzle part surprised me most. Cats are hunters. When food shows up in a bowl with zero effort, they have all this pent-up hunting energy with nowhere to go. A puzzle makes them work for it.

Not a sales post. I just put together a guide that covers the full 30-day enrichment plan, which puzzles work for which cat personalities, and the medical vs. boredom checklist. Happy to share the link if anyone wants it.
