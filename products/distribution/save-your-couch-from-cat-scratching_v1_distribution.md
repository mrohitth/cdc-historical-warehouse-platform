### Post Body:

I see a lot of posts here from people stuck in the same loop: cat scratches couch, try tape, try spray, try a scratching post that gets ignored, partner or roommate starts pushing for declaw.

I want to lay out what actually works, because the usual advice is incomplete.

**The real reason most solutions fail:**

Scratching posts don't work because they're usually wrong in one of three ways:
- Wrong material (carpet feels like a snare, not a shredder)
- Wrong stability (free-standing posts wobble — the couch doesn't)
- Wrong placement (stuck in a corner instead of near the furniture being targeted)

Cats don't scratch furniture because they're bad. They scratch because the couch is the most appealing surface available: stable, textured, central. The goal is to make a scratching post that competes.

**What actually works:**

Three coordinated approaches beat single-method attempts every time.

1. **Fix the scratching post.** Give the cat a sisal rope post that's either floor-fixed or heavily weighted (over 20 lbs). Place it within 3 feet of the couch, at the height the cat is actually scratching. Use the double-attractor method: put the post right next to the target, then slowly relocate it over weeks once the habit transfers.

2. **Deterrent on the couch.** During retraining (minimum 4 weeks), make the couch less appealing. Plastic floor mat nub-side up, double-sided cat tape, or a furniture cover all work. Don't remove it early — if the behavior regresses you'll be starting over.

3. **Nail caps as a bridge.** Brands like Soft Claws glue onto existing claws and render furniture damage impossible during retraining. They're temporary (4-6 weeks per application), non-invasive, and let the cat keep scratching naturally. Yes, they look funny. No, they don't hurt the cat.

**The declaw question:**

If you're being pressured to declaw — understand what it actually is. It's not nail trimming. It's surgical removal of the last bone of each toe. Chronic pain, arthritis, litter box avoidance, and increased biting are documented outcomes.

Declawing is banned in the UK, much of Europe, and a growing number of US cities. If someone in your household is pushing for it, they typically don't know what the procedure involves.
