**Title:** The real reason mice keep coming back after traps and exterminators

---

**Post body:**

Most people fight mice inside. That is why they keep losing.

Traps kill mice. They do not stop new ones from walking in through gaps you never found.

I spent time looking into how mice actually get into homes, and the number that comes up is consistently 1/4 inch — a mouse can fit through a hole the size of a dime. Most homeowners seal one obvious gap, miss six others that are smaller than they expected, and are confused when mice show up again a month later.

Here is the approach that actually works:

**1. The inspection rule**
Walk your foundation line at dusk with a flashlight. Look for gaps where pipes and wires penetrate your foundation, gaps where garage walls meet concrete (this one is missed constantly), and any crack along the exterior wall. Mark what you find with tape and come back to seal it.

**2. The sealing material hierarchy**
- Steel wool + caulk: the gold standard for gaps under 1/2 inch around pipes
- Expanding foam (pest-block formula): for larger voids around penetrations
- Hardware cloth: for gaps over 1/2 inch and vent openings
- Door sweeps: the cheapest, most effective fix for exterior doors — under $20

Do not use spray foam alone. Mice chew through it.

**3. The trap placement most people get wrong**
Traps go against walls, not in the middle of the floor. The trigger plate faces the wall so the mouse walks over it to reach the bait. Place them in corners, behind appliances, and near gaps you found and sealed.

Standard peanut butter works but dries out. A small piece of wax paper under the peanut butter holds it in place and the mouse has to work at it, which means more trigger time.

**4. The 14-day protocol**
Day 1-2: Full inspection + sealing. Day 3-5: Set traps. Day 6-10: Monitor and find any gaps you missed. Day 11-14: Confirm no new activity.

If you are still catching mice after 10 days, you have an active entry point you did not find. The flour test works well — sprinkle flour in front of suspected gaps overnight and check for footprints in the morning.

I put together a free printable checklist that walks through this room by room if that is useful. It is linked in my profile for anyone who wants it.

The homeowners who solve this permanently are not the ones with better traps. They are the ones who sealed the gaps.
