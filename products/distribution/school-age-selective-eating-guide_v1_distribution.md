## Reddit Bridge Post (r/Parenting)

**Title:** The selective eating problem no one talks about — the grandparent inconsistency issue

---

My 7-year-old son eats five foods reliably. Five. And one of them is ketchup.

I had a system. No short-order cooking. No grazing between meals. No short-circuiting with crackers because "he has not eaten all day." I was consistent, and we were starting to make progress.

Then Sunday happened. Grandma had him for the afternoon. Granola bars at 3pm. Pasta at 5pm because he "wouldn't eat anything else." By the time I picked him up, he had eaten six foods that are not on our plan — and was not hungry for dinner.

He looked at me at dinner and said: "But Grandma made me pasta."

He had learned. Not the food learning I wanted — the consistency exploitation I had worked hard to prevent.

This is the part of selective eating guides that nobody covers: the caregiver inconsistency problem. It is not just about your child's eating. It is about the system around your child.

Here is what I learned about addressing it:

**1. The problem is structural, not willful.**

Grandparents are not trying to undermine you. They are experiencing empathic pain when your child protests, and they solve their own discomfort by giving in. You need to help them understand that what feels kind is actually perpetuating the problem.

**2. Specific, bounded requests work better than general appeals.**

Not "please stop giving him food between meals" — but "on Sunday, no snacks after 3pm, and dinner is what we bring. If he does not eat it, that is okay. But we are not making something different."

**3. You need a co-caregiver alignment first.**

If you share parenting with a partner who has different rules, fix that before you fix anything with grandparents. My wife and I had to align on three non-negotiable rules before involving anyone else.

**4. The school-age angle is different from toddler picky eating.**

By age 7, your child has cognitive awareness of the inconsistency. He is not just refusing food — he is optimizing within a system he has mapped. This requires a more systematic approach than "just keep offering."
