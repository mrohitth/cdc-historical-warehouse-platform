**Body:**

My 9-year-old cat had been peeing on the couch for four months straight. Multiple vet visits. Bloodwork, urinalysis, ultrasound — all normal. The vet said she was healthy. I was at the point where I was seriously considering rehoming her because I couldn't keep dealing with it.

I spent three weeks doing exactly what everyone online says: I tried different litter. I added another box. I cleaned with enzymatic cleaner. Some of it helped. Most of it didn't.

What I didn't realize until I actually sat down and worked through it systematically: the standard advice ("add more litter boxes") doesn't tell you what KIND of box, what SIZE, what LITTER, or where to put it. Those variables matter more than the number.

Here's what I learned that actually moved the needle:

**The litter box audit (5 variables, not just "more boxes"):**
- Box size matters more than most people realize. Most commercial boxes are too small for an adult cat to turn around comfortably.
- Covered vs. uncovered changes behavior for many cats — try removing the cover for two weeks.
- Litter type preference varies by cat. Offer two boxes with different litter types and watch which one gets used.
- Location matters. Quiet, low-traffic, with a clear sight line to the room.

**The stress trigger question:**
The vet cleared her medically. That means the problem is behavioral. Behavioral problems have triggers. In our case it was neighborhood cats visible through the window — she'd been anxious for months and I didn't connect it. Once I blocked that view, the house soiling dropped by about 60% within a week.

**The enzymatic cleaner that actually works:**
Standard cleaners leave pheromone traces. Enzyme-based cleaners (Nature's Miracle, Rocco & Roxie) break them down. I used a blacklight to find spots I couldn't see and treated those too.
