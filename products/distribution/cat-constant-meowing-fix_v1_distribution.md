**Title:** What to do when your vet says "nothing's wrong" but your cat won't stop meowing

---

My cat meowed constantly for two years. Multiple vet visits, everything came back normal. I was told to just ignore it. That didn't work.

Here's what actually helped — the protocol that took me from "losing my mind" to "actually sleeping."

---

**1. Go back to the vet with a checklist, not a question**

When vets say your cat is fine, they usually mean "I didn't find anything on the basic exam." That doesn't rule out the subtle stuff.

Ask for:
- T4 thyroid panel (hyperthyroidism can cause increased vocalization before other signs show)
- SDMA kidney test (early kidney disease)
- Full chemistry panel
- Joint pain assessment

Hyperthyroidism and early kidney disease are both treatable. Don't miss them because the test wasn't run.

---

**2. Track the pattern before you change anything**

"Stop ignoring it" is advice that fails because it's too vague. You need to know: when does the meowing happen, what triggers it, and what does it get the cat?

For one week: log every episode. Time, what you were doing, what the cat did right before, what happened next.

Most people find a pattern. Often it's: cat meows → human responds → cat learns meowing works. The fix targets that loop specifically.

---

**3. Extinction only works when paired with replacement**

Ignoring alone doesn't work because your cat has learned that persistence eventually pays off. You have to:

a) Stop responding to meowing (zero eye contact, zero words, zero getting up)
b) Reward quiet behavior immediately — even brief silence gets acknowledged

The first two weeks get worse. The meowing intensifies before it gets better. This is normal. Push through.

---

**4. The environment check**

Does your cat have:
- A quiet feeding zone separate from the litter box?
- Multiple resting options at different heights?
- Consistent feeding times (same times daily)?
- 10-15 minutes of interactive play daily?

Cats with unpredictable routines and insufficient stimulation vocalize more. This isn't about being a "better" owner — it's about removing the underlying drive.

---

**5. If your cat is a vocal breed (Siamese, Bengal, etc.)**

Some cats are genetically more talkative. That's not behavioral — it's breed. The goal isn't silence, it's reducing unnecessary meowing and managing your expectations. A single "notification meow" before meals is realistic. Sustained 20-minute crying sessions may not be fully eliminable.
