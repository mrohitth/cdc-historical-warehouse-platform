**Post title:** My water damage claim was denied. Here's the appeal process that actually works.

**Post body:**

Had a pipe burst behind a wall. Insurance denied the claim citing "gradual deterioration." Spent three months fighting it. Eventually won on appeal. Here's what I learned:

**The denial letter is the beginning, not the end.** Most denials are written to sound final. They're not. The first step is reading the specific policy clause cited, then writing a formal appeal — not calling and arguing, but sending a written appeal via certified mail.

**Get a plumber's written report.** This was the single most important document. Your plumber needs to state in writing that the failure was sudden, not gradual, and explain why. A verbal conversation with an adjuster doesn't count. Written documentation does.

**Document everything.** Timestamped photos, itemized contractor estimates, maintenance records if you have them. Insufficient documentation is one of the most common denial reasons — and it's the easiest to fix on appeal.

**Appeals must be in writing, via certified mail.** Verbal calls go nowhere. Every communication needs to be in writing with delivery confirmation.

**The 30-day rule.** Most states require insurers to respond to formal appeals within 30 days. If they don't, file a complaint with your state insurance commissioner. That gets attention.

I put together a complete guide to the appeal process, documentation checklist, and alternative paths when insurance fails. It's free with a printable checklist.
