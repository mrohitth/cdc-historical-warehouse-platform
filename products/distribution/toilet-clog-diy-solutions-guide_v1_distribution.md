**What is a toilet auger?**

It's a short, specifically-designed snake that goes in your toilet (not a regular drain snake — those can crack porcelain). You crank it, it breaks up the clog. Done.

**Why your plunger isn't working:**

You're probably using the wrong type. A sink plunger (plain cup) doesn't seal properly on a toilet bowl's curved drain. You need a flange plunger — it has an extra piece that inserts into the drain opening.

**The quick hierarchy:**

1. Toilet flange plunger — works for ~60% of clogs (soft clogs, paper jams)
2. Toilet auger — works for ~85% of remaining clogs (hard clogs, foreign objects)
3. Plumber — reserved for main line issues or structural problems

The auger costs $25-40 at any hardware store. A plumber costs $150-300 for 10 minutes of work. The tool pays for itself the first time you use it.

**The technique that matters:**

When using an auger, insert at a 45-degree angle, rotate clockwise to extend, and when you feel resistance, keep rotating for a few more turns. On retract, rotate counterclockwise.

**Emergency no-tool option:**

Dish soap + hot (not boiling) water. Let the soap sit 15-20 minutes, then pour water from waist height. Works for soft clogs when you don't have a plunger.

What NOT to do:

- Don't use a regular drain snake in a toilet
- Don't use boiling water (thermal shock can crack porcelain)
- Don't keep flushing if it's clogged (overflow risk)
- Don't flush anything except toilet paper + human waste (yes, even "flushable" wipes — they aren't)
