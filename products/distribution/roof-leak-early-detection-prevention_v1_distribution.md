Saw a post on here recently from someone whose roof leak turned into $10K+ of attic damage before they even knew it was happening. Read through the comments and realized most people — myself included — don't have a system for checking their roof.

So I put together a seasonal roof inspection routine. It's not complicated, and you don't need to get on the roof. Here's what's in it:

**Attic check (from inside — no climbing):**
- Look at the rafters and underside of the sheathing with a flashlight. Water stains, dark discoloration, or soft spots = leak present.
- Check for daylight visible through the sheathing. If light gets in, water gets in.
- Smell for mustiness. Moisture problems show up as smell before they show up as visible damage.

**Exterior check (from ladder at gutter line):**
- Pipe boots — the rubber collars around vent pipes. These crack and fail more than any other single roof component. A cracked boot is the most common cause of residential roof leaks. You can see them from the ground with binoculars or from a ladder.
- Shingles — curling, cracking, or missing granules in gutters.
- Flashing — rust or gaps around chimneys, skylights, and wall intersections.

**Frequency:** Twice a year. Early spring after winter freeze/thaw. Early fall before cold weather arrives.

A permaboot (silicone pipe boot repair, $25-40, installs from a ladder) is probably the single most cost-effective leak prevention product a homeowner can buy for an aging roof.

Check your roof before next fall. Just took me 20 minutes this morning and I found one boot that needs replacing.
