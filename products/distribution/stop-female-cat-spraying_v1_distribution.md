I see a lot of posts here from owners dealing with a spraying cat, especially spayed females. The vet says she's fine. You've tried everything. I want to share what the research actually says works — not just anecdotes — so if you're in this boat, you're not spinning your wheels.

**Quick context:** I spent time looking at the actual studies and veterinary behaviorist guidance on this. Here's the framework that has the best evidence behind it.

**Step 1: Confirm it's spraying, not house soiling**
Spraying = small amounts on vertical surfaces (walls, doors), tail-up posture. House soiling = puddles on floors, squatting. These have different solutions. Make sure you're solving the right problem.

**Step 2: Medical ruling-out first — not just "vet says she's fine"**
Ask your vet specifically for:
- Urinalysis AND urine culture (culture catches hidden infections that dipstick misses)
- Physical exam including joint palpation if your cat is 7+
- Bloodwork if 8+

If you've only done a standard wellness exam, there's more to rule out. Some cats spray because something hurts (arthritis makes box access uncomfortable) or there's a low-grade UTI with no obvious symptoms.

**Step 3: Litter box audit — most owners skip this**
The standard: one box per cat plus one. Big, open-top bins (not standard small pans). Clumping, unscented litter. Boxes in quiet spots, not next to loud appliances.

If you have two cats and one sprays, you need three boxes minimum. Place them in different locations.

Top-entry boxes can actually make spraying worse for some cats — they can't back up and spray upward as easily, so they may spray outside the box instead. Try one big open bin first.

**Step 4: Feliway — yes, it has actual evidence**
The PMC meta-analysis (2012) found that pheromone therapy combined with other interventions produced the best outcomes. Give it 4 weeks minimum. One diffuser covers about 700 sq ft. Plug it in the room where spraying happens most.

**Step 5: Fluoxetine (Prozac) — if everything else failed**
If 4+ weeks of environmental changes and Feliway aren't working, talk to your vet about fluoxetine. Same study found that cats on fluoxetine had ≥90% reduction in spraying. It's off-label for cats but widely prescribed. Typical trial: 4-8 weeks before you judge whether it worked.

**Step 6: Enrichment — the piece most owners skip**
Daily play sessions (10-15 min), puzzle feeders, vertical space, hiding spots. A less bored, more physically tired cat has lower baseline stress. It compounds with everything else.
