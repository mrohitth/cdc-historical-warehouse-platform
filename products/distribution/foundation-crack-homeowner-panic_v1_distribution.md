**Post title:** Found a crack in my foundation — here's the order of who to actually call (not the contractor)

**Post body:**

Bought a house last year. Found a crack in the basement wall. Googled it at 2am and landed in full panic mode reading $80,000 repair stories.

What I learned after calling the right people in the right order:

**Step 1: Don't call a contractor first.** Foundation repair contractors have financial incentives to recommend work. The first call should be a structural engineer (PE license). They'll tell you if it's cosmetic or structural, give you a stamped report, and give you a scope that's based on reality rather than a sales pitch.

Cost: $500-$1,500 for the evaluation. Could save you $15,000 in unnecessary contractor estimates.

**Step 2: Measure the crack.** Get a digital micrometer ($15 on Amazon). Measure the width. Take a photo with a ruler. Do this monthly. If it's growing, that's the data point that tells you to escalate. If it's stable for 6 months, it's likely cosmetic.

**Step 3: Check your gutters.** Water is the primary driver of most foundation stress. Make sure downspouts exit at least 3 feet from the foundation. Make sure the ground slopes away from the house, not toward it. This costs almost nothing and fixes more than most people realize.

**Step 4: If repair is needed, get three bids.** With the engineer's report in hand, contractors compete on the same scope. Without it, each one will pitch different work.
