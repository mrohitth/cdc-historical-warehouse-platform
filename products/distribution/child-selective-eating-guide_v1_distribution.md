**Title:** What actually helped my child's selective eating (after years of mealtime battles)

**Post Body:**

My daughter has eaten approximately 7 foods for the past 3 years. Seven. Pasta with butter, goldfish crackers, applesauce pouches, chicken nuggets, rice, cucumber slices, and that's basically it.

We've done everything. Pressure ("just three bites"). Rewards ("finish your vegetables and you can have dessert"). Consequences. Short-order cooking. Short-order guilt. You name it, we tried it.

What I finally learned: selective eating isn't a compliance problem — it's a communication problem. My daughter wasn't refusing food to frustrate me. She was responding to sensory experiences, developmental stages, and anxiety in ways she couldn't articulate.

Here's what's helped us in the past 6 months:

**The Division of Responsibility actually works.** I serve the meal. My daughter decides what to eat from it. I stopped forcing bites, rewarding with dessert, and making separate meals. Within 2 weeks, the overall tension at meals dropped significantly.

**Non-pressured exposure is the key.** We put a tiny portion of a new food on her plate at every meal. No commentary. No "try it." No expectations. Just: "Here's dinner." After about 12 exposures to raw cucumber slices, she finally tasted one. It took 6 weeks. I almost gave up at week 3.

**Sensory profile matters more than I knew.** Turns out my daughter has significant texture sensitivities. The "just try it" approach was asking her to do something that felt genuinely overwhelming, not mildly unpleasant.

**We got medical input early.** Our pediatrician ran blood work that showed low iron. We addressed that medically while working on feeding behavior simultaneously.

I'm not saying we have a perfect eater now. We don't. But meals are calmer, she's trying more new foods, and I feel like I understand what's happening instead of just failing.

**Free checklist that helped me track progress:** There's a quick-reference guide that walks through the 20-food inventory and the expansion hierarchy. Having a concrete system made me feel less like I was just spinning my wheels.

Happy to answer questions about what worked for us specifically. This isn't medical advice, just sharing our experience.
