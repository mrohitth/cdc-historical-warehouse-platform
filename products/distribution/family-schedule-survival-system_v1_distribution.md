## Reddit Bridge Post

**Subreddit:** r/Parenting
**Title:** The one thing that finally stopped the "what's happening today?" loop
**Post:**

One parent carries the mental load of the family's schedule. Every practice, every appointment, every pickup — tracked in one person's head. Everyone else asks. Nobody else checks.

I found the fix through a combination of a wall calendar and a 20-minute Sunday habit. Here's what actually works:

**The wall calendar rule:** If it's not written down, it doesn't happen. Not orally, not verbally communicated — written on the calendar. Every family member can check it without asking anyone.

**The Sunday review:** 20 minutes before the week starts. Four parts: review this week, assign drivers, preview next week, kill anything that's not working. Families who do this have significantly fewer mid-week crises. It's not a productivity hack — it's structural.

**The one-in-one-out rule:** Every new activity means one existing activity comes off the calendar. Nothing is automatic. Everything is intentional.

**The check-first habit:** When someone asks you what's happening, you say "check the calendar." Every time. For at least the first month. It feels rude. It isn't.

The core insight: the problem is not that the schedule is too full. The problem is that one person is the only one who knows it. Making the information visible and holding the line on access fixes most of it.
