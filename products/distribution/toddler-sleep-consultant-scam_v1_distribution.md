**Title:** "I Paid $600 for a Sleep Consultant and Got a Template — Here's What I Learned"

---

**Body:**

I want to share something that took me a year to process because I'm guessing some of you are where I was two years ago.

I hired a sleep consultant when my daughter was 22 months old. She'd never been a great sleeper, but after a regression at 18 months, I was running on maybe 4 hours a night for four months straight. I was desperate. I had no village. I was starting to resent my own kid.

The consultant cost $600 for a "comprehensive" package. She did a 45-minute intake call. She asked detailed questions about feeding, schedule, temperament. She made me feel like this was truly personalized.

Then she sent me a PDF.

The PDF was the chair method. I'd already tried the chair method. I'd found it on Google for free.

I'm not sharing this to complain about the money, although the money stung. I'm sharing because I spent the next six months implementing advice that wasn't designed for a toddler with separation anxiety. My daughter got worse. The consultant told me I wasn't being consistent enough.

That was a lie.

Here's what I wish someone had told me before I hired anyone:

**The sleep consulting industry is unregulated.** Anyone can call themselves a certified sleep consultant. There's no board, no licensing, no standard. The "certifications" you see consultants list are typically weekend courses that cost a few hundred dollars.

**Most consultants use three templates.** Ferber, extinction, or chair method. The "personalized" intake is used to upsell you on a more expensive package, not to customize anything. The testimonials on their Instagram are survivorship bias — the parents who saw no improvement don't get featured.

**Toddlers are not infants.** The methods that work for a 6-month-old often don't just fail for a toddler — they can cause harm. A 6-month-old cries because they don't understand object permanence. A 22-month-old cries because they know you're choosing to leave them alone. Those are different things.

After I stopped the sleep training, I spent three months rebuilding trust with my daughter. Three months of going to her every time she cried, no questions. Three months of not trying to "fix" anything.

She's 3.5 now. She sleeps through the night. Not because I trained her. Because she was ready, and she trusted me again.

If you're considering a sleep consultant, please do this first:

1. Download the free AAP sleep guidelines
2. Read the r/sleeptrain wiki (it's actually good)
3. Ask the consultant these 7 questions before paying anything (I'll paste them in a comment)

And if you already hired someone and it didn't work — it wasn't you. It was the template.

I'm happy to answer questions about my experience. I'm not here to sell anything. Just wish someone had told me this two years ago.

7 questions to ask a sleep consultant before paying:
1. What is your medical training background?
2. How do you adapt your approach for separation anxiety in toddlers?
3. What do you do if the method isn't working after 2 weeks?
4. Do you have peer-reviewed research backing your approach?
5. Can I speak with previous clients who had a toddler with separation anxiety?
6. Do you require payment upfront? What's your refund policy?
7. Will you share the exact protocol in writing before I pay?

If they dodge any of these, walk away.
