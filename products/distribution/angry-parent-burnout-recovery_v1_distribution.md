**Title:** "I'm not a bad parent. I'm an unregulated parent. Here's the difference — and what to do about it."

**Body:**

I'll be direct: I yell. Not constantly, not dangerously — but more than I want to, more than I planned to, and it is taking a toll.

For a long time, I thought the solution was to try harder. To be more patient. To catch myself before it happened. What I didn't understand was that I was trying to out-willpower my own nervous system — and that doesn't work.

Here's what I've learned that actually helps:

**1. The preconditions are not optional.**
Hungry, tired, and touched-out parents have measurably shorter fuses. Not because they're weak — because the biology is real. Eating before you're hungry, building 90-second breaks into your day, and recognizing your personal warning signs before you cross the threshold are the foundation. Everything else sits on this.

**2. "Just don't yell" doesn't work. "Why are you yelling" is the wrong question.**
The right question is: what happened 30 seconds before the trigger that put the system in a place where yelling was the only option left? Usually: skipped meals, accumulated overwhelm, a child behavior that read as intentional defiance when it was actually developmental. Address the preconditions, not the reaction.

**3. The repair conversation is not optional — and there's a wrong way to do it.**
Yelling will happen. How you repair it matters enormously. The over-apology ("I'm so sorry, are you okay?") puts your emotional state onto the child and teaches them they need to manage you. The non-apology ("I wouldn't have yelled if you had just listened") justifies the yelling. The repair script is brief, specific, and accountable without being destructive.

What you say: "I yelled. That was my voice getting bigger because I was overwhelmed. It was not because of anything you did wrong."

**4. If you have intrusive thoughts about harming your child — this is an urgent signal, not a character verdict.**
These thoughts are more common than parents admit. They are distinct from intent. They are a symptom of a nervous system at its absolute limit. Call your doctor or the Postpartum Support International helpline (1-800-944-4773). This is exactly what professional support is for.
