**Title:** Cats and new babies: what I wish someone had told me before I brought my daughter home

**Post:**
This is going to be long, and I apologize in advance. But I'm posting because I spent my entire pregnancy terrified about how my cat would react to a baby, and the internet gave me nothing but jokes and horror stories.

We have a 4-year-old female tabby named Mochi. She's never been aggressive toward humans — the worst she's done is swipe at ankles when she wants food. When I got pregnant, everyone kept saying "just wait until the baby comes, you'll have to rehome her." That terrified me. Mochi is family.

Here's what actually happened:

**Before the baby arrived (6 weeks out):**
- Set up the nursery and installed a baby gate Mochi couldn't jump (45"+). She learned the room was off-limits before there was a baby in it.
- Started scent desensitization — let her sniff baby blankets and onesies from friends' babies. Did NOT force it.
- Moved her litter box to a room the baby can never reach.
- Set up a cat tree in the living room with a high perch so she could observe without being involved.
- Started a "special time" ritual — 10 minutes of wand toy play every evening at the same time.

**The first meeting:**
One parent held the baby. The other was in the room with Mochi behind a baby gate. We kept it under 10 minutes. Mochi hissed once and retreated to her high perch. Treats for calm behavior. We ended there.

**After the first week:**
Mochi was still avoiding the baby room. Fine. We let her set the pace. She slept on our bed for the first time in months — she had more vertical space in the living room now that the baby gear was set up.

**Where we are now (4 months in):**
Mochi is not friends with the baby. She's not even close. But she tolerates the baby's existence. She has her spaces. The baby has her spaces. We've had zero incidents.

**The one rule I never break:** Mochi and the baby are never in the same room without active supervision. Not for one second.

What I wish I'd known: most of the "cat attacked baby" stories I've read involve zero environmental management. Cats don't randomly attack infants — they show stress signals first. The owners ignored them or didn't know what to look for.

If you're pregnant with a cat, the work starts now. Not after the baby arrives. Now.
