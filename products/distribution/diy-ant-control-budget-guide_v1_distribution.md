**Body:**

Three weeks ago I watched a thread in here where someone said they couldn't afford an exterminator and were going crazy watching ants march across their kitchen every morning.

I've been dealing with ant problems in old homes for years and I kept seeing the same mistakes. So I put together a free checklist for exactly this situation.

Here's what most people get wrong:

**1. You're killing the wrong ants.**
The ants you see are workers — they can't start a new colony, they can't end one. Spray kills what's on the counter. The queen is still in the wall.

**2. You quit too early.**
Bait stations take 7-14 days to collapse the colony. Most people remove them after 2 days because "they're not working." They are working. You're just not waiting.

**3. You didn't close the door.**
Treating inside without sealing entry points means ants find a new route. A $3 tube of exterior caulk along your baseboards does more than most people realize.

What actually works:
- Identify the ant species first (different species need different baits)
- Use slow-acting bait so workers carry it back to the nest
- Seal the entry points with caulk and steel wool
- Apply an exterior perimeter treatment

I put together a free printable checklist that walks through the whole 14-day protocol. It's designed for the budget homeowner — everything costs under $30.
