**Post Title:** The maintenance tasks that matter most for new homeowners — a seasonal breakdown

**Post Body:**

I bought my first house 14 months ago. Within 30 days, I realized I had no idea what I was doing. The previous owner was great, but no one hands you a checklist that actually says "here's what costs $50 to fix now and $5,000 to fix later."

So I built one. After a lot of research, contractor conversations, and one genuinely expensive mistake (long story, involved my water heater), I put together a seasonal maintenance guide for new homeowners.

Not a 200-item list. Just the stuff that matters, in the seasons that matter.

**What I wish I'd had on day one:**

The 5 tasks that never wait:
1. Know where your main water shut-off is — a burst pipe costs you thousands per minute you can't find it
2. Test smoke detectors monthly — 2 minutes, saves lives
3. Label your electrical panel — when something trips at 11pm you don't want to guess
4. Find your sewer cleanout — sewer backups are not something you want to figure out during one
5. Flush your water heater annually — sediment kills water heaters early, and a new one is $800-2,000

**The seasonal approach changed everything for me:**

Spring = Reset. HVAC tune-up, roof inspection, gutter clean. Winter breaks things — find them before rainy season.

Summer = Systems. Irrigation audit, garage door service, septic scope if your house is older.

Fall = The important season. Gutter cleanup is the one task I will never skip again. Furnace prep before you need heat. Weatherstripping while it's still warm enough to have windows open.

Winter = Monitor. Filter changes, ice dam watching, water heater flush once a year in December or January.

**The $500 rule:** Any repair over $500, get three written estimates. Not because contractors are dishonest — but because one quote is an opinion, three quotes is a market.

I've made this into a free guide (the seasonal breakdown is the core of it). No sales pitch — just the system that keeps a first-year homeowner from making the expensive mistakes I made.

**What I won't tell you:** This isn't a replacement for a home warranty or a good inspector. It's just the maintenance side of things that the closing paperwork doesn't cover.

If you're a new homeowner and overwhelmed, start with the five non-negotiables above. Everything else can wait until you have those handled.

AMA about the first year of homeownership. The things I got wrong would fill a small book.
