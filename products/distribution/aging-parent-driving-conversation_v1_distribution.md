## Reddit Bridge Post (r/AgingParents)

**Title:** How I finally got my mom to agree to stop driving — what worked and what did not

---

My mom is 82 and has been driving since I was in high school. Two years ago, I started to get worried. She scraped the car twice in one month. She got lost going to a friend's house she had been to for 20 years. She admitted to a near-miss on the highway that she laughed off.

I did not know how to bring it up. Every conversation I imagined ended badly.

What I finally learned: this is not a single conversation. It is a process. And the parents who successfully give up driving are the ones who feel like they chose to stop, not like they were forced.

Here is what worked:

**1. I stopped starting with "you need to stop driving."**

I started with: "I've been worried about something and I want your input." That shifted the dynamic. I described specific incidents I had observed. I asked her how she felt about her driving. I let her reflect before I made my point.

**2. I had a transportation plan ready before I raised the topic.**

The biggest mistake I see people make: they go to the conversation with "you cannot drive" and nothing to replace it. I spent two weeks researching senior transportation in her area. When I raised the topic, I could say: "Here is what getting around would actually look like." That made it a solvable problem, not an amputation.

**3. I used her doctor.**

At her next physical, I asked the doctor to evaluate her driving fitness. The doctor — not me — said she recommended my mom stop driving. My mom was much more receptive to that than to me saying it.

**4. I handled the grief.**

When she finally agreed to stop, she went through a genuine grief period. I made sure she had things to do, places to go with others, reasons to leave the house. She is actually happier now than she was when she was driving — because driving had become stressful for her, even if she would not admit it.

This is a hard conversation. Happy to share what I learned if anyone is in the middle of it. There is a free guide that goes into much more detail — including a script for the first conversation and a full transportation planning worksheet.
