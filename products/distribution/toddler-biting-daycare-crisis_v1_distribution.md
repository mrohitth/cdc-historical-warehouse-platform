**Title (for reference):** What actually worked for us: the protocol we wish someone had given us during our daycare biting crisis

**Post Body:**

My son was 22 months old when his daycare director called us in for a meeting. He had bitten another child for the third time in a month. We left work early, sat in a conference room, and felt completely lost.

Here is what we learned through that experience — not as experts, but as parents who had to figure this out from scratch.

**1. Biting at this age is almost always communication.**

Not malice. Not a character flaw. A child who does not have enough words trying to tell you something. The American Speech-Language-Hearing Association confirms this. A toddler with 15 words and a lot of opinions does not have a behavioral problem. They have a communication gap.

**2. The 5 bite types matter more than the incident.**

When we finally tracked what was happening *before* each bite, a pattern emerged. For us, it was frustration biting — another child took a toy and my son had no words to protest. When we identified that, we could teach "mine" and "help" signals. The biting did not stop overnight, but it started to drop within three weeks.

**3. What to actually do in the first 60 seconds (and what not to):**

Do: Move to the bitten child. Stay calm. Say "Teeth are not for biting. Biting hurts." Then later, with your own child: "You can say help."

Do not: Bite them back (teaches that biting is okay). Lecture in the moment (they cannot retain it). Put them face-to-face to apologize (adds stress without teaching).

**4. Ask your daycare for an incident log — and share it with your pediatrician.**

We started keeping track of every incident. What happened before, where in the room, what the trigger was. After two weeks, we could see it was happening almost exclusively during free play when another child took a toy. That pattern changed how we approached it.

**5. Rule out physical causes before assuming it is behavioral.**

Ear infections, constipation, teething — all of these lower frustration thresholds significantly and are easy to miss. Ask your pediatrician specifically about these if your child's biting has increased suddenly.
