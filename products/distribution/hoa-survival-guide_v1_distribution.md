HOAs gone bad are one of the most consistently discussed topics on these subreddits, and for good reason — once a board shifts culture, you're trapped unless you know the playbook.

I want to share what I found useful after digging into this for my own situation. This isn't a sales post — I'm just sharing the framework that helped me understand what options actually exist.

**The Three Moves That Actually Work:**

**1. Read Your Documents First**
Most homeowners have never read their CC&Rs or Bylaws. The board's authority lives and dies by those documents. If they cite a rule that isn't in writing, that rule may not be enforceable. Get the actual documents and read the fine print on enforcement procedures.

**2. Board Elections Are Won by the Organized**
HOA board elections have some of the lowest turnout in American civic life. Most seats are won with a small number of votes — often fewer than 50. If you and 10 neighbors show up and vote together, you can take control. The math is almost always in favor of whoever actually shows up.

**3. Document Everything From Today Forward**
Every fine, every notice, every interaction. Write down dates, what was alleged, what document section was cited (if any). If you end up in a legal dispute or need to sell, this paper trail is everything.

The specific details vary a lot by state — some states have strong homeowner protections, some give boards near-total authority. But the three moves above apply everywhere.
