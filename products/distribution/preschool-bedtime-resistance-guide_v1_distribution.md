**Title:** What actually works for bedtime battles with a strong-willed 5-year-old? (Not "just be consistent")

**Post body:**

Bedtime with my 5-year-old has become a nightly nightmare. We've tried being consistent. We've tried "just walk away." We've tried charts and rewards. Nothing sticks for more than a few nights.

The problem isn't that I don't know I need to be consistent. The problem is that my kid is smart about it — they know exactly what to say to get me back in the room, and I'm realizing I'm助纣为虐 by not having a better script.

I finally found something that works better than "just be consistent" (which, by the way, is real advice but not very actionable at 8pm when you're exhausted).

**The three-phrase script that changed bedtime for us:**

When my kid calls out after I've said goodnight, I used to have a whole conversation. Now I just say:
1. "It's bedtime."
2. "I love you."
3. "I'm staying close."

That's it. I say it once. I leave within 30 seconds. I don't explain, justify, or add anything.

What this does: it gives my kid the reassurance they actually need (I'm here, I love you) without giving them a reason to stay awake (no negotiation, no explanation). The boundary is the bedtime — not my patience.

**The check-in system that doesn't backfire:**

If my kid escalates (crying, yelling, getting out of bed), I wait 2-3 minutes, go to the room, stand by the door, say the three phrases once, and leave within 90 seconds. I don't sit on the bed. I don't linger. I don't add hugs "just to make sure they're okay."

This took about two weeks to work consistently. The first few nights were rough. But now bedtime is... not a nightmare. I still have to hold the boundary, but I don't have to have a whole debate about it.

**The parent composure piece nobody talks about:**

Here's what I had to admit: my kid's bedtime resistance was partly my fault because I was escalating. When I started doing a 60-second reset before bedtime (three breaths, drop my shoulders, set an intention), I showed up calmer. And my kid responded to that.

If you're at your limit every night, that's not sustainable. You have to regulate yourself first. The research on this is actually pretty clear — parent arousal level affects child compliance more than any parenting technique.

Happy to answer questions. I know what it's like to feel like you're failing at something this basic.
