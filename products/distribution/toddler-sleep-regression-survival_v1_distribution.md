**Body:**

That post from a few days ago about the parent who said "I hate who I've become with my 3 year old" hit different. 325 upvotes in a day. Every parent in that thread knew exactly what they were talking about.

Here's the thing about sleep regressions that nobody puts in the parenting books:

**The reason sleep regressions are so brutal is that you're not dealing with a sleep problem. You're dealing with a development problem.**

Your toddler's brain is literally reorganizing itself during sleep. They're learning to climb, or run, or talk in sentences — and it doesn't stop when the sun goes down. The 2am practice sessions aren't them being difficult. It's their brain processing.

The cruelest part: overtired toddlers produce cortisol. That same hormone that keeps you wired after too much coffee. More tiredness → more cortisol → harder to fall asleep → more tiredness.

This is why "just try an earlier bedtime" backfires so often during regressions.

**What actually helps during a regression (as opposed to what makes it worse):**

**DO:**
- Keep wake time consistent. Wake time matters more than bedtime during regressions.
- Respond to night wakings faster than you normally would. Shorter responses = less full waking.
- Keep the existing routine. Don't add new steps or drop the ones that work.
- Use the 5-minute rule for night wakings (wait before responding — many are partial arousals where they'll resettle on their own).

**DON'T:**
- Start sleep training during a regression. Your child is at their limit. This is not the time.
- Let the bedtime drift later because "at least they're finally asleep." This creates an overtired spiral.
- Make major parenting changes. You're exhausted and your judgment is compromised.

The regressions pass. The 18-month, 2-year, and 2.5-year regressions typically resolve in 2-4 weeks. What you do during them matters for how quickly they end — but they always end.
