**Title:** My 14-year-old deaf cat yowls at 1am, 3am, and 5am every night. Here's what's actually helped.

**Body:**

Three months ago, my cat started yowling at night — not the polite meow she always had, but this loud, almost mournful sound. She's 14 and went deaf last year, and somewhere in the past few months, something else changed too. The vet said bloodwork looks fine. But she can't sleep through the night, and neither can I.

I spent weeks trying the standard advice: white noise, calming plugins, ignoring it. Nothing worked, because the standard advice is for attention-seeking behavior in young cats. My cat wasn't trying to manipulate me. She was confused and possibly in pain, and I was treating it like a behavior problem when it was a medical one.

Here's what actually changed things:

**1. Go back to the vet — but ask for specific tests.**
Senior blood panel, T4 thyroid test, blood pressure, arthritis palpation. Hyperthyroidism is extremely common in cats over 10 and causes exactly this kind of nighttime vocalization. Arthritis affects the vast majority of cats over 12 and worsens at night when the house is cold and surfaces feel harder. These are both treatable.

**2. Night lights.**
It sounds absurdly simple, but cats with cognitive decline (essentially feline dementia) navigate poorly in the dark. A $6 plug-in night light near her litter box, in the hallway, and near her bed changed her ability to find her way at 3am. She wasn't yowling because she wanted attention — she was lost.

**3. Pheromone diffusers.**
Feliway Optimum, specifically. It takes 2-3 weeks to build effect, but it noticeably reduced her nighttime anxiety.

**4. Heated bed.**
Arthritic cats are in more pain when they're cold. A heated cat bed (the kind with a microwavable disc inside or an electric heating pad) gave her somewhere warm to settle instead of pacing and crying.

**5. Feed her main meal at night, after a short play session.**
Building sleep pressure before bed, then feeding, mimics the natural hunt-catch-eat cycle. It genuinely helped her sleep longer stretches.

The yowling hasn't stopped entirely — she's 14, and this is a chronic condition. But it's reduced from constant to occasional, and I can sleep again. More importantly, I stopped feeling guilty for being frustrated, because I finally understood what was actually happening.

If your senior cat has started crying at night, don't assume it's behavioral. Get the medical workup first.
