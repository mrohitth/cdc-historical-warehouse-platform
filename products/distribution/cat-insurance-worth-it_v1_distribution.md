## Reddit Bridge Post

**Title:** "Did the math on cat insurance vs. self-insuring. Here is what I found."

**Subreddit:** r/CatAdvice

**Post Body:**

Three years ago I bought pet insurance for my cat. Last month I actually ran the numbers to see if it was worth keeping.

Here is the short version: for most cats, it depends on your cat's age, breed, and health history. The math is not the same for everyone.

I built a spreadsheet to calculate break-even point. Took my annual premiums × expected cat lifespan, compared against statistically likely vet costs.

A few things I learned that surprised me:

1. Premiums feel cheap monthly ($30-50) until you add them up over 10-15 years. Then it is $4,000-$8,000 in total payments.

2. Breed matters a lot. Persians, Maine Coons, Siamese — these breeds have hereditary conditions that insurance actually covers. For mixed-breed cats, self-insuring often wins.

3. The age 8 cliff is real. Most providers raise rates 20-30% when your cat turns 8. After that, the math often breaks.

4. Pre-existing conditions are excluded permanently by most providers. If your cat already has a diagnosis, switching providers will not help.

I ended up keeping the insurance for my 2-year-old mixed-breed rescue for now, but I will re-run the math at age 6.

Not a sales post. Just putting the framework out there because I wish someone had explained it to me before I signed up.

**What I wish I had before buying:** A guide that explains the break-even calculation, provider comparison criteria, and age-based strategy. I found one that covers all of this if anyone wants the link — happy to share.
