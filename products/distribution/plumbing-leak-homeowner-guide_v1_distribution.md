**Title:** I spent 3 hours researching before calling a plumber for a "small leak" — here's what I learned (and what would've saved me time)

**Post:**

Three weeks ago I noticed a slow drip under the kitchen sink. I didn't know if it was something I could fix or something that would turn into a $3,000 slab leak.

So I did what any non-plumber does: I watched YouTube videos at 11pm, got conflicting advice, and eventually called a plumber — not because I was sure I needed one, but because I didn't know HOW to know if I needed one.

The plumber showed up, tightened something in 5 minutes, charged me $175 for the service call, and left. It worked fine for two weeks, then the drip came back. Different plumber, $150. Same thing happened again.

I eventually figured out I was calling too late on some things and too early on others — and spending way more than I needed to because I had no framework for assessing what I was actually dealing with.

I put together a simple classification system for plumbing leaks (slow seep vs. active drip vs. pressure leak vs. hidden leak vs. slab leak) and a 7-point severity calculator that tells you whether to DIY, schedule a pro within 72 hours, or call one right now.

It would've saved me about $400 in repeated service calls and a lot of anxiety.

The thing I wish I'd had at the start: a checklist that tells you exactly what type of leak you have, what it means, and what to do next. It's free. Sharing it here in case it helps someone in the same position I was in.
