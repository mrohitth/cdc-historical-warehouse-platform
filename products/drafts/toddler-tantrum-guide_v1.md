# From Meltdown to Calm: A Parent's Field Guide

## Overview
This guide helps parents understand and navigate toddler tantrums with practical, field-tested strategies for moving from meltdown to calm.

## Understanding Toddler Tantrums
Toddler tantrums are normal developmental behaviors. They occur because toddlers lack the emotional regulation and language skills to express frustration. The key is staying present without trying to "fix" everything.

## The CORE Method
- **C**alm presence: Stay regulated yourself
- **O**utward listening: Validate without fixing
- **R**egulation support: Help them breathe
- **E**mpty space: Let it naturally subside

## What NOT to Do
- Don't try to reason during the peak
- Don't give in to demands made during tantrums
- Don't physically restrain unless dangerous
- Don't shame or humiliate

## After the Storm
Talk about what happened when everyone is calm. Help your toddler name their feelings and practice words for next time.

## When to Seek Help
If tantrums are causing injury, lasting more than 15 minutes regularly, or feel qualitatively different (glassy eyes, seeming disconnected), consult your pediatrician.

## Quick Reference Card
1. Stay calm — they need your regulation
2. Don't try to fix — just be present
3. Protect their dignity — this is not personal
4. Wait out the storm
5. Connect after — re-regulation before re-engagement
## Conclusion

Toddler tantrums are a normal part of development — not a sign of bad parenting. Your job isn't to stop them but to guide your child through them with calm, steady presence.

Remember the CORE method: **Calm presence, Open listening, Regulation support, and Empty space to let the storm pass.**

When in doubt, stay regulated yourself. Children learn to manage big emotions by watching you model it.
