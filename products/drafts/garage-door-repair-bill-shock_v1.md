*How to diagnose the problem, compare contractor quotes, and make the repair-vs-replace call — without getting taken.*

## Introduction: Why Garage Door Repair Feels Like a Scam (And Sometimes Is)

Your garage door is stuck. You called a company. They came out, spent 15 minutes looking at it, and handed you a quote for $2,800. You don't know if that's reasonable. You don't know what the door actually needs. You don't know if the part costs $200 or $2,000. All you know is you need your door to work and you need it now.

This is the garage door repair trap. It's not always a scam — sometimes $2,800 is genuinely what a repair costs. But the industry is structured in a way that punishes homeowners for not knowing things they have no reason to know. This guide fixes that.

By the end of this guide, you will:
- Know what actually broke in your garage door system
- Know what fair prices look like for common repairs
- Know how to get quotes that compete against each other
- Know when to repair and when to replace

This is not a DIY guide. Garage doors are under extreme tension and can cause serious injury. But knowing what you know is the difference between signing a $2,800 quote you didn't need and signing a $400 repair that actually fixed the problem.

## Chapter 1: How to Figure Out What Actually Broke

### Before You Call Anyone

Most homeowners call a garage door company the moment the door won't move. That means the contractor arrives, tells you what's wrong, and you accept it — because you don't have a frame of reference.

Here's the 5-minute self-diagnosis you can do before you call:

**Step 1: Does the door lift manually?**
Pull the emergency release cord (it's a red handle hanging from the center track). Now lift the door by hand. If it lifts smoothly and stays open at mid-height, the springs are intact. The problem is in the opener or the motor. If the door is heavy — really heavy, like you shouldn't be lifting it — the springs are gone.

**Step 2: Does the motor run but the door doesn't move?**
If you hear the motor running but the door isn't going anywhere, the issue is the drive system. This could be the gear and sprocket assembly (common in chain-drive openers) or the belt (in belt-drive openers). Parts cost $30-$80. Labor to replace is $150-$250.

**Step 3: Does the door close and then immediately reopen?**
This is almost always a sensor issue. There are two sensors at the bottom of the door — one sends an infrared beam, the other receives it. If the beam is interrupted or misaligned, the door thinks something is in the path and reverses. A contractor will sometimes diagnose this as a circuit board problem and charge $300 for a board replacement. Misalignment is a 5-minute fix you can do yourself.

**Step 4: Do you hear a grinding or popping noise?**
Grinding during opening usually means the sprocket or drive gear is wearing. Popping can mean the torsion springs are fatigued. Both are real problems — but they don't always mean you need a full system replacement.

### What Garage Door Components Actually Cost to Fix

| Component | Typical Part Cost | Typical Labor | Common Overcharge Pattern |
|-----------|-------------------|--------------|--------------------------|
| Extension spring | $20–$60 each | $100–$150 | Quoted in pairs at premium markup |
| Torsion spring | $80–$250 each | $150–$250 | "Upgraded spring" adds $200–$400 |
| Opener motor | $150–$400 | $100–$200 | Bundled pricing hides the markup |
| Drive gear / sprocket | $30–$80 | $100–$200 | Diagnosed as motor failure |
| Sensors (pair) | $15–$40 | $75–$150 | Overdiagnosed as circuit issue |
| Circuit board | $80–$200 | $100–$200 | The most overdiagnosed part |
| Cables | $30–$80 | $100–$200 | Quoted with springs unnecessarily |

The numbers above are your internal reference. When a contractor quotes you, ask them to itemize each of these components.

### The Sensor Fix (Do This First)

Before you call anyone for a door that closes and immediately reopens: look at the two sensors at the base of the door frame. They're small boxes about 6 inches off the ground, one on each side. Loosen the mounting bracket on the sensor side, adjust the sensor until the small indicator light on each unit turns solid green (not blinking), and retighten. In 50% of these calls, this is the entire problem.

Cost if it works: zero.
Cost if you called a contractor first and they diagnosed it as a circuit board: $300–$800.

## Chapter 2: Getting Quotes That Actually Compete

### The Minimum: Three Written Bids

For any garage door repair over $300, get three written bids. This isn't overkill — it's how you know the first bid is reasonable.

When you call contractors, here's what to say:
> "I have a [describe problem: e.g., broken torsion spring, door won't open]. I want a written quote that breaks down parts and labor separately. I am getting two other estimates."

Contractors who refuse to itemize, who give only verbal quotes, or who pressure you to decide immediately — walk away.

### The Questions That Matter on Every Quote

**1. What exactly is broken?**
Ask for the part name, not the symptom. "The mechanism" is not an answer. "Torsion spring, model [X]" is an answer.

**2. What is parts cost, and what is labor?**
If a contractor says "it's $800 total," push for the split. If parts are $600 and labor is $200, that's very different from parts being $100 and labor being $700.

**3. If you recommend replacement, what would repair cost?**
Ask this every time a contractor recommends replacing the whole system. The answer should be specific and grounded. If they can't tell you what repair would cost, they may be recommending replacement because it's more profitable.

**4. Do you have a service call fee, and does it apply to the final bill?**
Many companies charge $75–$150 just to come out and diagnose. Some apply this to the bill if you proceed. Others don't. Know this upfront.

**5. Do you mark up parts, or pass cost at cost?**
Some contractors add 30–50% markup on parts. Others pass cost at cost and make their margin on labor. Either model is fine — you just want to know which one you're dealing with.

### The Angi's / HomeAdvisor Problem

Third-party lead generation platforms charge contractors 20–40% of the job cost to connect them with you. That commission has to come from somewhere. Contractors on these platforms quote higher to absorb the platform fee. This doesn't mean the contractor is dishonest — it means your quote includes a hidden tax of up to 40%.

Going direct — finding a contractor through a neighborhood Facebook group, a NextDoor recommendation, or a local garage door company with a Google listing — gets you pricing without the platform tax.

### Red Flags in Any Garage Door Quote

- **"We always recommend replacing the whole system when we see this"** — Legitimate only for units older than 20 years. Anything younger should be evaluated for targeted repair.
- **"Add-ons while we're here"** — "You should replace the weather seal" or "your tracks look worn" are often add-on upsells not related to your actual problem.
- **Refusal to provide written quote before work** — This is a hard line. Walk away if they won't put it in writing.
- **No bid competition**: One contractor, one quote, "this is just how it is." Garage door repair is not a monopoly.

## Chapter 3: Repair vs. Replace — Making the Call Rationally

### When Repair Makes Sense

**The door is under 15 years old and only one system is failing.**
If the opener is shot but the door itself is fine, replace the opener. At $300–$600 installed, a new opener is almost always cheaper than the repair quote on an old motor.

**The springs failed but the door is structurally sound.**
Spring replacement is routine. A door with working panels, straight tracks, and functioning rollers just needs springs. Replacing the whole door would cost $1,500–$4,000 more than the $950 spring replacement you actually need.

**You have documentation of regular maintenance.**
If you've had the door serviced annually and can show records, a contractor should be giving you a discount on repair — not padding the bill because they found something wrong.

### When Replacement Makes Sense

**The door is over 20 years old and multiple systems are failing.**
At a certain age, you're one more $800 repair away from the next emergency. Replacing the door — even at $2,500–$5,000 installed — ends the cycle.

**The door is damaged beyond cosmetic repair.**
Dented panels, warped sections, or a door that no longer sits level puts uneven stress on the entire system. A new door solves this and adds curb appeal.

**You want a modern WiFi opener with camera and app control.**
These cost $250–$500 installed. If your current opener is dying anyway, this is the right time to upgrade.

### The Hidden Cost Saver: Supply the Opener Yourself

If the problem is the opener motor, buy the unit yourself from Home Depot or Amazon ($150–$400 depending on model). Then pay a flat labor rate ($75–$125) for installation. Contractors who bundle "opener + install" typically charge $500–$900 for a $250 opener. Doing it yourself saves $250–$500 on this one line item.

Not all contractors will accept this arrangement — some won't warranty work on parts they didn't supply. But it's worth asking. The ones who say yes are often the more experienced independent operators.

## Chapter 4: The Maintenance That Prevents Emergency Calls

Garage doors need maintenance twice a year. Most homeowners never do it. This is why the spring that should have lasted 12 years failed at 8.

**Every 6 months:**

1. Lubricate the moving parts: Use a silicone-based lubricant on the torsion springs, hinges, and rollers. Do not use WD-40 — it cleans but doesn't lubricate.

2. Tighten the hardware: The vibration of a garage door cycling thousands of times per year loosens bolts and brackets. Walk the full length of the track and tighten any loose bolts.

3. Check the weather seal: The rubber seal on the bottom of the door cracks over time. Replacement seal costs $20–$40 and takes 20 minutes to install.

4. Test the balance: Pull the emergency release and lift the door to mid-height. It should stay put. If it creeps upward or falls, the springs are losing tension and need attention before they fail completely.

A 20-minute twice-yearly maintenance routine extends the life of every component in your garage door system.

## Conclusion: You Are Not Helpless

The garage door industry profits from your helplessness. That's not a conspiracy — it's just the natural result of an industry where most customers transact once every decade, contractors have technical knowledge you don't have, and urgency prevents comparison shopping.

This guide gave you the knowledge. Use it.

Get three bids. Ask for itemized quotes. Verify the diagnosis before you authorize any work. Buy your own opener if the motor is the issue. Maintain the door twice a year.

You don't need to become a garage door expert. You just need to know enough to not get charged $12,000 for something that should have cost $950.
