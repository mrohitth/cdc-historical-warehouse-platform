## PROBLEM DIAGNOSIS

**Core Problem:** Adult and senior cat owners whose cat has been to the vet and received a clean bill of health but continues to eliminate outside the litter box — on beds, couches, laundry piles, or floors. The vet visit confirmed no UTI, no kidney disease, no diabetes. The owner is left with a behavioral problem they don't know how to solve, and standard advice ("add more litter boxes," "try a different litter") hasn't worked. They're frustrated, embarrassed, and some are considering rehoming.

**Root Cause Cluster:**
1. **Litter box set-up mismatch** — the standard "one box per cat" rule often fails for house-soiling cats; size, location, box type, and litter substrate all factor in but most owners haven't systematically tested these variables
2. **Stress/anxiety trigger** — environmental change (new pet, new baby, moved furniture, construction noise, neighborhood cats outside) triggers anxiety-driven house soiling that mimics revenge but is actually communication
3. **Surface preference** — some cats develop a preference for soft surfaces (beds, laundry) over litter, especially after a medical issue or a single bad experience with the litter box
4. **Multi-cat household dynamics** — bullying at the litter box forces subordinate cats to find alternative places to eliminate

**Who This Is For:** Cat owners who have already taken their cat to the vet for a house-soiling problem, received a clean bill of health, tried switching litter and adding boxes, and still have a cat peeing outside the box. They're at the end of their rope and need a systematic diagnostic process, not random tips from Google.

**Differentiation from existing catalog:**
- `stop-female-cat-spraying` → territorial marking on vertical surfaces (walls, doors), driven by stress/threat perception
- This product → elimination on horizontal surfaces (beds, floors, couches), after medical causes ruled out. Different presentation, different diagnostic approach.

## SOLUTION PILLARS

### Pillar 1: Litter Box Optimization Audit (Verified)
**Claim:** Most house-soiling cats live in a litter box setup that would fail a basic audit. Number of boxes, box size, location, and litter type all contribute — and most owners haven't systematically tested these variables.
**Evidence:** Cornell University Feline Health Center and ASPCA recommendations state that litter box issues are the #1 cause of house soiling in cats. The guidelines are: one box per cat plus one, uncovered, large enough for the cat to turn around comfortably, located away from food and high-traffic areas.
**Status:** VERIFIED — supported by Cornell Feline Health Center and ASPCA.

### Pillar 2: Environmental Stressor Identification (Plausible)
**Claim:** House soiling in vet-cleared cats is often triggered by environmental changes or stressors that owners don't recognize as significant. A systematic review of recent changes (even minor ones) can identify the trigger.
**Evidence:** Veterinary behaviorists (Journal of Feline Medicine and Surgery) confirm that stress and anxiety are primary drivers of non-medical house soiling. However, identifying specific stressors is case-by-case and not well-studied at scale.
**Status:** PLAUSIBLE — well-established in veterinary behavior literature, but individual triggers are highly variable.

### Pillar 3: enzymatic Deodorant + Reintroduction Protocol (Verified)
**Claim:** Standard cleaners do not stop re-marking. Only enzymatic cleaners break down the pheromone markers that draw a cat back to the same spot. Combined with a gradual reintroduction to the litter box, this can retrain the cat's preference.
**Evidence:** Multiple veterinary sources confirm that cats follow scent trails. Enzyme-based cleaners (Nature's Miracle, Rocco & Roxie) break down uric acid and pheromones. Standard soap and water leave traces.
**Status:** VERIFIED — supported by veterinary cleaning literature.
