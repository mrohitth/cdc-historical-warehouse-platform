# SKELETON: Gas Water Heater Pilot Light Repair Guide

## Problem Diagnosis

A gas water heater with a pilot light that won't stay lit means no hot water. For homeowners, this is an immediate crisis — showers, dishwashing, laundry all depend on it. Plumbers charge $150-400 for service calls where the actual repair is often a $15 part and 30 minutes of work.

**The core frustration:** The information gap. Forum posts are either too vague ("check the thermocouple") or too technical (wiring diagrams for gas valves). Homeowners don't know if they should spend $15 or $1,500. They don't know if their water heater is dying or just needs a $15 part.

**Why pilot lights go out:**
- **Thermocouple failure** — The most common cause. The thermocouple is a safety device that detects whether the pilot is lit and keeps the gas valve open. If it's faulty, the gas shuts off even if the pilot is burning. Thermocouples degrade over time, especially in areas with hard water.
- **Dirty orific** — Dust and debris accumulate in the pilot orifice over time, blocking the small flame needed to keep the thermocouple hot.
- **Gas supply issues** — Low gas pressure or a partially closed gas valve can extinguish the pilot.
- **Ventilation problems** — A downdraft from wind or a blocked flue can blow out the pilot light.
- **Faulty gas valve** — Least common but most expensive. If the gas control valve itself is failing, the entire water heater may need replacement.

**The emotional component:** Homeowners feel stupid and vulnerable. They don't want to get scammed by a plumber. They're afraid of gas leaks. They don't know if the water heater is old enough to replace outright.

**The gap:** No guide walks homeowners through a systematic diagnosis, explains what each part does, gives clear fix vs. replace thresholds, and offers the emotional reassurance that this is doable.

## Solution Pillar 1: The 5-Minute Home Diagnostic Sequence
*(VERIFIED: standard troubleshooting from plumbing codes and manufacturer guides)*

Before buying any parts, confirm what's actually wrong. This diagnostic sequence costs nothing and takes 5 minutes.

**Step 1 — Check the obvious:**
- Is the gas valve fully open? (Handle should be parallel to the pipe)
- Is there a "pilot" button you need to hold down while lighting?
- Is the knob on the water heater turned to "pilot" and not "off"?
- Is there a flashing indicator light? (Read the error code on the water heater's label)

**Step 2 — Check for a downdraft:**
- Is there wind coming down the flu pipe? (Go outside, visually inspect)
- Is the draft hood (the metal bell shape at the top of the water heater) blocked?
- Is there a bathroom fan, dryer, or range hood that could be creating negative pressure?

**Step 3 — Smell test for gas leaks:**
- Do you smell gas near the water heater when the knob is at "pilot"?
- Do you smell gas at the gas valve connection points?
- If yes to gas smell: do NOT proceed. Call your gas company or a licensed plumber.

**Step 4 — Relight protocol (document the exact steps):**
- Turn the knob to "off"
- Wait 5 minutes for gas to clear
- Turn knob to "pilot"
- Hold down the pilot/reset button while using a long match or butane lighter
- Keep the button pressed for 60 seconds after lighting
- Release and observe: does the pilot stay lit?
- If it goes out immediately: thermocouple is almost certainly the issue
- If it stays lit briefly then goes out: could be thermocouple or draft issue

## Solution Pillar 2: The Step-by-Step Thermocouple Fix
*(VERIFIED: standard DIY plumbing repair — universally recommended when thermocouple is the cause)*

**What to buy:**
- Universal thermocouple: $8-15 at any hardware store (Home Depot, Lowe's, Ace)
- Bring your water heater's brand and model number if unsure of fit
- You do NOT need a brand-name part — universal thermocouples work for 95% of residential gas water heaters
- Also buy a can of compressed air or a small bottle of rubbing alcohol and cotton swabs (for cleaning the orifice)

**Tools needed:**
- Adjustable wrench (or basin wrench if in a tight space)
- Screwdriver
- Possibly a 1/4" nut driver

**Safety first:**
- Turn gas to OFF at the valve
- The water heater will have standing pilot — the gas remaining in the line is minimal but work in a ventilated area
- No open flame while working

**Replacement steps:**
1. Remove the access cover/burner cover at the bottom of the water heater
2. Locate the thermocouple — it's a thin copper tube with a tip that sits in or near the pilot flame
3. The thermocouple connects to the gas control valve with a simple push-in or screw-on connection
4. Disconnect it from the gas valve (no tools needed for the connector — just pull or unscrew)
5. Remove the old thermocouple from the pilot assembly — usually held by a clip or screw
6. Insert the new thermocouple into the same position — the tip must be in the pilot flame zone
7. Reconnect to the gas valve
8. Turn gas back on and relight the pilot using the steps from diagnostics
9. Wait 2-3 minutes to confirm the main burner ignites and stays lit

**Common mistake:** The new thermocouple tip is not positioned in the flame. The flame should wrap around the tip — if it's too far away, it won't generate enough voltage to keep the gas valve open.

## Solution Pillar 3: When to Repair vs. Replace — The Honest Decision Guide
*(PLAUSIBLE: standard heuristics from trade sources and home inspection literature)*

**Factors that push toward replacement:**
- Water heater age: If your water heater is over 12-15 years, the tank lining is likely corroding (check for rust-colored water). A new water heater costs $800-1,500 installed — cheaper than repeated repairs.
- Sediment buildup: If flushing the tank (run a hose into the drain valve) produces rust and debris, the tank is degrading from the inside.
- If the gas valve is bad: Gas control valves cost $150-300 — at that point, evaluate whether replacing the whole unit makes more sense.
- If you smell gas and can't find the source: Do not repair. Replace.

**Factors that support repair:**
- Water heater is under 10 years old
- You have hard water and haven't been flushing the tank annually
- The repair is a thermocouple ($15) or simple cleaning
- You have a tankless water heater: these have different components and repair costs but specific models have known issues — check your model

**The emotional trap:** Homeowners often replace too early (out of fear of gas) and also wait too long (hoping a cheap fix will work on a dying unit). The honest heuristic: if your water heater is over 10 years old and needs a second repair in 5 years, replace it. If it's 6 years old and needs a $15 part, repair it.

**When in doubt:** Call a licensed plumber for an assessment (not repair) — many will do a diagnostic visit for $75-100, which is cheaper than a full service call and gives you a professional opinion to act on.
