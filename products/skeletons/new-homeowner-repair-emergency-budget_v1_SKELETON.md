# New Homeowner Repair Emergency Budget — Skeleton v1

**Trend Source:** r/Homeowners (December 7, 2025)  
**Raw Quote:** "Wait until something breaks and you don't have the money to fix it but you need it. So frustrating!"  
**Total Score:** 8.3/10

## Problem Statement

New homeowners discover that ownership comes with a continuous stream of expensive repairs they never budgeted for. A $3,000 HVAC call. A $1,500 plumbing emergency. A $500 appliance failure. Without an emergency fund or cost knowledge, they get hit with credit card debt or delayed repairs that cost more later.

## Solution Pillars

### Pillar 1: The 1% Rule — How Much You Actually Need to Save
**Fact Check: VERIFIED**

Industry standard: save 1–2% of your home's value annually for maintenance and repairs:
- $400,000 home = $4,000–$8,000 per year = $333–$667/month
- First-year homeowners typically encounter deferred maintenance from the previous owner
- Major systems (HVAC, plumbing, roof) have 15–25 year lifespans — replacement costs $5,000–$20,000

**Action:** Open a separate savings account labeled "Home Repair" and set up automatic transfer on payday. Start with $200/month even if you can't hit the full 1% yet.

### Pillar 2: Triage Framework — Emergency vs. Can Wait vs. Preventive
**Fact Check: VERIFIED**

Not all repair calls are equal:
- **True emergencies (call immediately):** Gas leak, broken pipe flooding, no heat in winter, sewer backup, roof opening in rain
- **Urgent but not emergency (within 1–2 weeks):** HVAC failure in shoulder season, water heater failure, broken appliance
- **Can wait 30–90 days:** Cosmetic repairs, non-functioning garbage disposal, minor plumbing leaks with a bucket underneath
- **Preventive this year:** Gutter cleaning, HVAC tune-up, water heater flush, chimney inspection

**Action:** Create a home repair triage list. When something breaks, classify it before calling anyone. This prevents $200 emergency plumber fees for a $20 DIY fix.

### Pillar 3: Getting Fair Quotes Without Getting Scammed
**Fact Check: VERIFIED**

Home repair is a high-fraud industry:
- **Get 3 quotes minimum** for any repair over $500. Never accept a single quote as final.
- ** Yelp and Google Reviews** — look for patterns (repeated complaints vs. one-offs)
- **Angie's List / HomeAdvisor / Thumbtack** — get 3 screened contractors
- **Red flags:** Upfront payment over 30%, no written contract, pressure to sign same day, quote that seems "too good to be true"
- **Scope of work:** Always get a written itemized scope of work. "Fix plumbing" is not a scope. "Replace 20 feet of cast iron pipe with copper, including permits and wall repair" is.
