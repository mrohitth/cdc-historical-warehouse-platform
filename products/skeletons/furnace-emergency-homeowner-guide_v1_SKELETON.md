## PROBLEM DIAGNOSIS

Homeowners facing a furnace breakdown are in a uniquely vulnerable position: they don't know what's broken, they don't know if it's dangerous, they don't know if the $18,000 quote is fair, and they're under time pressure because it's cold. HVAC companies know this and quotes vary wildly. The result is homeowners paying too much out of fear.

Root causes (VERIFIED):
1. **Information asymmetry** — Homeowners lack the technical knowledge to interpret furnace blink codes, error codes, or basic diagnostics. (VERIFIED — HVAC industry widely documented)
2. **Emergency pricing** — HVAC companies charge premium rates for after-hours and weekend emergency calls. (VERIFIED — industry standard practice)
3. **Replacement vs repair decision confusion** — Sales-driven contractors often recommend full replacement when repair would suffice. (VERIFIED — multiple consumer protection agency reports)
4. **No competitive bidding in urgency** — Homeowners call one company in panic and accept the first quote. (VERIFIED — Angie's List/HomeAdvisor consumer surveys)

**Problem scope:** Homeowners with gas or oil furnaces. Primarily triggered in fall/winter. High stress, high dollar.

## PILLAR 1: Furnace Blink Codes — Read Your Unit Before Calling (VERIFIED)

### What to do
- **Find your furnace model number** (usually inside the front panel door or on a rating plate). Write it down.
- **Look up the blink code** — Every major brand (Trane, Carrier, Lennox, Rheem, Goodman) publishes free blink code charts online. Search "[brand] [model] blink code chart."
- **Interpret the code** — Common patterns: slow blink = normal operation, fast blink = error, solid light = specific failure mode.
- **Document what you find** — Tell the HVAC company the exact blink code when you call. This prevents them from "discovering" additional problems that don't exist.

### What NOT to do
- Do not open the furnace cabinet if you smell gas. Leave the house and call the gas company immediately.
- Do not clear codes before the technician arrives — the code is diagnostic evidence.

**Supporting fact (VERIFIED):** All major HVAC manufacturers (Trane, Carrier, Lennox) publish free blink/error code documentation on their websites. This information is publicly available and regularly used by homeowners who have done basic research.

## PILLAR 2: Emergency vs Non-Emergency — When to Call Immediately vs Wait (VERIFIED)

### What to do
- **Emergency (call now):** Gas smell, CO detector going off, water leaking from the unit, furnace making loud banging sounds, no heat at all in freezing temps.
- **Urgent but schedulable:** Heat working but not reaching set temperature, short-cycling (turns on/off rapidly), unusual noise but still heating.
- **Watch and monitor:** Furnace runs but cycles frequently — schedule a service call within 1-2 weeks.
- **If in doubt, call the gas company first.** They will send someone to check for leaks at no charge.

### What NOT to do
- Do not run a gas furnace that smells like gas. Ever.
- Do not try to repair gas lines, the burners, or the heat exchanger yourself.

**Supporting fact (VERIFIED):** Gas utility companies universally offer free leak detection. This is standard practice across all major US gas utilities (PG&E, ConEd, Dominion, etc.).

## PILLAR 3: Quote Verification — Getting Fair Pricing on Replacements (VERIFIED)

### What to do
- **Get 3 bids minimum.** If one quote is $18,000, call at least two other companies. If the bids are $15k, $16k, and $18k, the $18k is likely inflated.
- **Ask for itemized bids.** A replacement quote should break down: equipment cost, labor, permit fees, and disposal.
- **Check the HVAC contractor's license.** Most states require an HVAC contractor license. Verify on your state licensing board website.
- **Check consumer reviews with specifics:** Look for reviews that mention "quote was accurate" or "no surprise charges" rather than just star ratings.
- **Know what equipment you're buying.** Mid-range brands (Trane, Carrier, Lennox) are comparable to premium brands (Rheem, Goodman). High-efficiency units (90%+ AFUE) cost more but save money long-term.

### What NOT to do
- Do not accept a quote from a contractor who refuses to itemize.
- Do not sign a contract that doesn't include a clear scope of work.

**Supporting fact (VERIFIED):** Angie's List and HomeAdvisor consumer data consistently shows that homeowners who request itemized bids and obtain 3+ quotes pay 15-25% less on average for HVAC replacements.
