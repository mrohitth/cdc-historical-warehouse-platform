## PROBLEM DIAGNOSIS

**Core Problem:** Cat owners in apartments face apartment-wide destruction — walls, door frames, carpet, and baseboards — because their cats have large or strong claws that shred surfaces. The standard solution (claw caps) fails because owners don't know proper sizing, reapplication schedules, or application technique. The result is mounting damage, security deposit losses, and emotional guilt.

**Root Cause Cluster:**
1. **Claw cap failure from sizing/application errors** — Wrong size caps fall off immediately; cats pull them off when stressed; owners don't reapply every 4-6 weeks
2. **Apartment-specific surfaces** — Walls, door frames, and low-pile carpet have different textures than furniture. Cats target these areas for territorial marking and stretching
3. **Renter constraints limit solutions** — No drilling, no permanent modifications. Standard advice (cat trees, double-sided tape on furniture) doesn't translate well to rental walls and shared spaces
4. **Under-stimulation driving displacement scratching** — Apartment cats often have less vertical space and territory, increasing stress-driven scratching

**Who This Is For:** Apartment-dwelling cat owners facing cat damage to walls, doors, and carpet; owners who have tried claw caps without success; renters worried about security deposit losses.
