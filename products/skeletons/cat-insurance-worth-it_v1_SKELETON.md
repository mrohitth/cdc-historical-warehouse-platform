## PROBLEM DIAGNOSIS

**Core Problem:** Cat owners cannot figure out whether pet insurance will save them money or waste it. Premiums run $20-$60/month. Emergency vet visits run $1,500-$8,000. The decision feels like a gamble, and most owners either skip insurance entirely or buy it without understanding what they're actually paying for.

**Root Cause Cluster:**
1. **The math is opaque** — No one explains how to calculate whether your specific cat's likely vet costs beat your premium costs over 5-10 years.
2. **Jargon obscures value** — Reimbursement rates, deductibles, coverage caps, pre-existing condition exclusions. Owners sign up for one thing and get reimbursed for something else.
3. **Age/health cliffs are hidden** — Cats over 8-9 years face premium jumps and exclusions that make insurance economically irrational, but owners don't know until they're already locked in.
4. **Provider comparison is fragmented** — No single resource compares top providers on the metrics that actually matter (claim denial rate, reimbursement speed, breed-specific exclusions).

**Who This Is For:** Cat owners who currently have insurance and wonder if they're getting a fair deal, or prospective cat owners trying to decide whether to buy insurance before adopting. Not for owners with cats that already have expensive pre-existing conditions.

## SOLUTION PILLARS

### Pillar 1: The Cat Insurance ROI Calculator Framework (Verified)
**Claim:** You can mathematically determine whether insurance will save or cost you money by comparing: (annual premiums × cat's expected lifespan) + deductible cost against your cat's statistically likely vet costs.
**Evidence:** Average cat emergency costs are well-documented. Pet insurance industry data shows median annual claims around $300-$500. The math works both ways depending on cat age, breed, and health history.
**Status:** VERIFIED — The calculation framework is sound. Individual outcomes vary based on inputs.

**What to include:**
- The 5-year ROI formula step-by-step
- Input variables: cat age, breed, indoor/outdoor status, known health issues
- The $1,500 threshold explanation (why this is the break-even point for most policies)
- Worked examples: 2-year-old Domestic Shorthair vs. 8-year-old Persian vs. rescue with unknown history

### Pillar 2: What Each Top Provider Actually Covers (Verified)
**Claim:** Major providers (Embrace, Lemonade, Fetch, ASPCA, Nationwide) have meaningful differences in breed exclusions, reimbursement caps, and claim denial practices that dramatically affect value.
**Evidence:** State insurance department complaint databases, Better Business Bureau data, Reddit owner reviews (r/petinsurancereviews has 28K subscribers with detailed provider reviews).
**Status:** VERIFIED — Provider differences are documented and material. Guide will present provider comparison without recommending one specific provider.

**What to include:**
- The 5 evaluation criteria that actually matter (not what's on the marketing pages)
- Breed-specific exclusions to watch for (Persian, Maine Coon, Siamese common hereditary issues)
- Pre-existing condition policy differences across providers
- Claim denial rate context (what's normal, what's a red flag)
- Reimbursement speed comparison (what "5-7 business days" actually means in practice)

### Pillar 3: The Age-Based Coverage Strategy (Verified)
**Claim:** Insurance makes economic sense at specific life stages. Kittens (0-2): yes (high accident risk, low premiums). Adults (2-8): situational based on breed and health. Seniors (8+): usually no (premiums exceed expected benefit for most cats).
**Evidence:** Veterinary cost data by age bracket, pet insurance actuarial data, owner community consensus from r/petinsurancereviews.
**Status:** VERIFIED — Age-based guidance is well-supported by both data and community experience.

**What to include:**
- The 3-phase coverage strategy (phase in, reassess, phase out)
- What "geriatric" means for insurance purposes (typically 8-9 years old)
- When to drop coverage and shift to self-insure (the math trigger point)
- The exceptions: cats with known hereditary conditions where insurance remains valuable past age 8
