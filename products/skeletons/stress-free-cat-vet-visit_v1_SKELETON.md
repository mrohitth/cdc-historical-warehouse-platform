## Problem Diagnosis

Taking an aggressive cat to the vet feels impossible. The moment the carrier comes out, the cat hides. The car ride is screaming and clawing. At the clinic, the cat is so amped up that vets can't examine them — sometimes the appointment gets cancelled mid-way. This creates a dangerous cycle: the cat's negative associations compound, and routine preventive care falls by the wayside. Untreated, this leads to missed vaccines, undetected illness, and expensive emergency care down the line.

**Root causes identified:**
1. Cats are territorial — leaving the home is inherently stressful
2. Carrier = "bad experience" because it's only associated with vet visits
3. No pre-visit pharmaceutical intervention (most owners don't know gabapentin is an option)
4. Clinic environment amplifies fear (dogs, strange sounds, restraint on metal tables)

---

## Solution Pillar 1: Carrier Conditioning (VERIFIED)
**Claim:** Systematic carrier desensitization can make the carrier a safe space rather than a threat trigger.
**Evidence:** Fear Free℠ feline handling guidelines, American Association of Feline Practitioners (AAFP) stress reduction protocols. Cat behaviorists universally recommend leaving the carrier out year-round with bedding and treats.
**What to cover:**
- Leave carrier out 24/7 with door open, favorite blanket, catnip
- Feed meals near and eventually inside the carrier
- Practice short car rides (just sit in driveway, then drive around the block)
- Never force the cat in — use treats and patience
- Carrier types that reduce stress (top-load, large enough to stand)

---

## Solution Pillar 2: Gabapentin Pre-Visit Protocol (VERIFIED)
**Claim:** A single dose of gabapentin 60–90 minutes before the vet visit significantly reduces stress and aggression in cats.
**Evidence:** Per a 2017 JAVMA study, gabapentin was found safe and effective for reducing feline stress and aggression at the vet. Also supported by dvm360 and peer-reviewed pharmacologic reviews (MDPI 2025). Widely used off-label by vets.
**What to cover:**
- Ask your vet about a gabapentin prescription (typically 50–100mg for average adult cat)
- Give 60–90 minutes before departure
- Monitor at home — some cats get wobbly or sedated
- Don't double up if you miss the window
- Contraindications (kidney disease — discuss with vet first)
- What to expect: calmer cat, still aware, easier to examine

---

## Solution Pillar 3: At-Home Vet and Mobile Vet Alternatives (PLAUSIBLE)
**Claim:** For cats too aggressive to transport safely, at-home vet services and mobile vets are a viable alternative.
**Evidence:** Mobile vet services exist in most metropolitan and suburban areas. Lap of Love, VetSeOut, and similar services offer full exams, vaccines, bloodwork, and euthanasia at home. Not available everywhere — rural availability is limited.
**What to cover:**
- Mobile/in-home vet services: Lap of Love, your local equivalent
- What they can do: full physical exam, vaccines, blood draws, nail trims
- What they can't do (surgery, X-rays requiring equipment)
- Tele-triage for判断ing whether an in-person visit is needed
- Cost comparison: typically 1.5–2x clinic pricing but saves stress and transportation risk
