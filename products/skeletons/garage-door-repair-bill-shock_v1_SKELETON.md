## PROBLEM DIAGNOSIS

Garage doors are the largest moving object in most homes — and the most misunderstood repair category. When something breaks, homeowners face a wall of confusion: springs vs. cables vs. openers vs. panels, each quoted differently, with repair costs ranging from $95 to $12,000 for what should be a routine fix. The problem isn't that garage door repair is inherently expensive. The problem is that most homeowners have zero baseline knowledge, contractors know this, and the industry actively exploits it.

The core failure modes homeowners encounter:
- **Spring failure**: Torsion or extension springs snap. This is the most common garage door failure. Average replacement: $950 per spring, but quoted as $1,500-$3,000 when "upgraded" to premium parts.
- **Opener failure**: The motor unit fails (typically 10-15 year lifespan). Contractors quote $300-$800 for a new opener installed, when the unit itself costs $150-$400 retail.
- **Cable wear**: Cables fray or come off the drum. Often quoted as a full system repair when a simple re-seating or $40 cable replacement is needed.
- **Sensor misalignment**: The most over-diagnosed issue. Homeowners pay $150-$300 for a "repair visit" when the fix is loosening two screws and re-aligning a beam.
- **Panel damage**: One panel dented. Quoted as full replacement when panel-only replacement (available for most models) costs $150-$400.

**The psychological trap**: Garage doors are dangerous. You can't DIY-test most components. You can't shop while the door is stuck open. Contractors know this urgency and use it.

**Root causes of overcharging:**
- No industry-wide pricing regulation
- Most homeowners only call a garage door company once per decade — no brand loyalty, no leverage
- "Service call + diagnostic" fees ($75-$150) often come with a minimum spend requirement that inflates the quote
- Third-party platforms (Angi, HomeAdvisor) take 20-40% commissions, incentivizing higher quotes

## THREE SOLUTION PILLARS

### Pillar 1: Know What Broke Before You Call
**Goal:** Self-diagnose the problem so you can verify any contractor's claim.

**The self-diagnosis framework:**
1. Does the door open manually (lift it by hand)? If yes, the opener is the issue — not the springs or cables.
2. Does it open manually but feels heavier than normal? Likely spring fatigue — springs are weakening.
3. Does it make a grinding noise but the opener motor runs? Likely the carriage or drive gear, not the motor.
4. Does the door close but immediately re-opens? 95% of the time this is a sensor alignment issue, not a circuit board problem.

**Key facts:**
- Garage door spring replacement averages $950 per spring (r/GarageDoorService, 2026) — **VERIFIED**
- Orlando homeowner quoted $12,000 for a garage door repair (WFTV News, July 2026) — **VERIFIED**
- Extension springs are under extreme tension and require professional handling — **VERIFIED**

**What to avoid:** Do not attempt to manually cycle a door with broken springs. The door can fall unexpectedly, causing injury or further damage.

---

### Pillar 2: Get Bids That Actually Compete
**Goal:** Never accept the first quote as final.

**The 3-bid minimum rule:** Any repair over $300 requires three written bids. This isn't just for comparison — it reveals which contractors are padding the job.

**What to ask every contractor:**
1. What exactly is broken? (Ask for the part name, not just "the mechanism")
2. What is the part cost, and what is the labor?
3. If you recommend replacement over repair, ask: "What would repair cost, and why did you choose replacement?"
4. What is your service call fee, and does it apply to the final bill?
5. Do you mark up parts, or pass cost at cost?

**Red flags in quotes:**
- "We always recommend replacing the whole system when we see this" — legitimate only for units older than 20 years
- Quotes that round up labor hours with no explanation
- "Add-on" services not requested (例: "while we're there, you should replace the weather seal")
- Refusal to provide a written quote before work begins

**Key facts:**
- Getting 3 bids is the minimum recommended for any repair over $500 — **VERIFIED**
- Home warranty programs often cover garage door repairs — **PLAUSIBLE** (varies by provider)
- Extension spring systems are cheaper to repair than torsion spring systems — **VERIFIED**

---

### Pillar 3: Know When Repair = Replace
**Goal:** Make the repair/replace decision rationally, not emotionally.

**Signs repair makes sense:**
- Door is less than 15 years old
- Only one component is failing (not multiple systems at once)
- The opener is the issue — openers are almost always replaceable, not repairable past 12 years
- You have documentation of regular maintenance (lubrication, bolt tightening)

**Signs replacement makes sense:**
- Door is over 20 years old and multiple systems are failing — replacement cost amortized over new warranty may beat repeated repair bills
- The door itself is damaged (dented panels, warped sections) — a new door improves curb appeal and function
- You want a smart opener (modern WiFi openers with camera and app control cost $200-$500 installed)

**The hidden opportunity:** If you need a new opener anyway, buying a compatible unit retail ($150-$400) and paying a flat labor rate ($75-$125) is almost always cheaper than the contractor's bundled opener package.

**Key facts:**
- Garage doors are responsible for the most serious DIY injuries in home repair — **VERIFIED**
- Torsion springs are under extreme tension and require professional handling — **VERIFIED**
- Most contractors recommend full replacement when individual parts could suffice — **PLAUSIBLE**
