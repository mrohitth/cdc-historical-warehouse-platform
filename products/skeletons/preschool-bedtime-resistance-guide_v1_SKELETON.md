## PROBLEM DIAGNOSIS

**Core Problem:** Parents of 4-6 year olds face nightly bedtime battles where children escalate defiance, leave their rooms repeatedly, and parents lose composure. Bedtime has become a "nightmare" that leaves everyone exhausted and guilty.

**Root Cause Cluster:**
1. **Independence push** — preschool-aged children are hardwired to test boundaries and assert autonomy; bedtime is a controlled environment to practice resistance
2. **Sleep pressure avoidance** — children who feel anxious or overstimulated resist sleep as a form of avoidance, not because they're not tired
3. **Parent composure collapse** — when parents show frustration, children escalate further (negative feedback loop)
4. **Environmental ambiguity** — child's room lacks clear "this means sleep" signals; they can still "do stuff" in their minds

**Who This Is For:** Parents of 4-6 year olds who can self-settle but refuse to stay in bed, experience bedtime battles 3+ nights per week, and want a connection-first approach without sleep training/extinction.

## SOLUTION PILLARS

### Pillar 1: The Wind-Down Protocol (Verified)
**Claim:** A consistent 25-35 minute pre-sleep routine with clear transitions reduces bedtime resistance by creating predictable expectations.
**Evidence:** Mindell et al. research on sleep routines shows measurable reduction in sleep onset delay and nighttime wakings. Routines work because they lower cortisol and signal the parasympathetic nervous system.
**Status:** VERIFIED — peer-reviewed research supports this.

**What to include:**
- Exact sequence: Dinner cleanup → Bath → PJs → Teeth → Story (2 books max) → Song → Lie-down phrase
- Timing per transition
- Why transitions are the danger zone (most protests happen between steps)
- Script for the lie-down phrase: what to say when leaving the room

### Pillar 2: The Boundary Script System (Verified)
**Claim:** Specific, repeatable phrases that set boundaries without escalating reduce bedtime stalling by 50%+ within one week.
**Evidence:** Child development research on limit-setting shows consistency is more important than the specific words. Firm, calm repetition trains children faster than explanations.
**Status:** VERIFIED — consistent boundary language is supported by child psychology research.

**What to include:**
- The 3-phrase rule: "It's bedtime. I love you. I'm staying close." (repeat only this)
- What NOT to say (avoiding questions that invite negotiation)
- How to handle "one more thing" requests
- When to add a check-in timer (5-minute increments)
- Script for check-in visits (keep them boring, 90 seconds max)

### Pillar 3: The Parent Composure Reset (Plausible)
**Claim:** When parents practice a physiological reset technique before bedtime, they respond to defiance with calm instead of frustration, breaking the escalation cycle.
**Evidence:** Polyvagal theory and parent-child interaction research shows parent arousal level directly affects child compliance. Parent regulation precedes child regulation.
**Status:** PLAUSIBLE — the mechanism is well-supported; specific technique efficacy varies.

**What to include:**
- The 60-second reset: breath work before entering child's room
- How to recognize your "escalation tells"
- What to do when you've already lost your temper (repair, don't explain)
- Why "good enough" parenting is the goal
