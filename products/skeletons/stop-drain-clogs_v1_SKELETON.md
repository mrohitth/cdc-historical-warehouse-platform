## PROBLEM DIAGNOSIS

**Core Problem:** Homeowners deal with recurring drain clogs (sinks, showers, tubs) despite using drain cleaners and plungers. Clogs keep returning every few months, wasting money on products that only partially work and eventually requiring expensive plumber calls.

**Root Cause Cluster:**
1. **Soap scum accumulation** — Bar soap (sodium tallowate) leaves residue that builds up over 6-8 months, narrowing pipes
2. **Hair + oil combo** — Bathroom drains clog from hair + body oils bonding to pipe walls, not just hair alone
3. **Improper drain cleaner use** — Chemical cleaners sit on top of clogs instead of clearing them; some damage pipes
4. **No preventive maintenance** — Clogs are only addressed after they fully form, not before

**Who This Is For:** Homeowners with recurring slow drains or clogs in bathroom sinks, showers, or tubs who want to stop the cycle without calling a plumber every few months.

## SOLUTION PILLARS

### Pillar 1: The Soap Scum Reset (Verified)
**Claim:** Switching from bar soap to liquid body wash + monthly enzymatic drain treatment prevents the soap-scum buildup that causes recurring clogs.
**Evidence:** Plumbers confirm bar soap leaves residue that narrows pipes over time. Enzymatic cleaners (not chemical) eat organic buildup safely.
**Status:** VERIFIED — mechanism confirmed by plumbers and drain professionals.

**What to include:**
- Why bar soap causes buildup (sodium tallowate + minerals = scum)
- Recommended enzymatic cleaners (BioClean, Green Gobler)
- Monthly maintenance protocol (15 min, no tools)
- What to do if buildup is already severe (snaking first, then maintenance)

### Pillar 2: The Hair Trap System (Verified)
**Claim:** Installing and maintaining hair catchers + monthly hair removal from drain cover eliminates the #1 cause of bathroom drain clogs.
**Evidence:** Plumbers consistently cite hair as primary clog cause in bathroom drains.
**Status:** VERIFIED — universal agreement among drain professionals.

**What to include:**
- Best hair catcher types (tub stopper vs. flat drain cover)
- How to clean without removing the catcher (2-min weekly task)
- Deep clean method for existing hair clogs (dismantling drain stopper)
- When to snake vs. when to call a pro

### Pillar 3: The Drano-Free Protocol (Plausible with nuance)
**Claim:** Chemical drain cleaners are less effective than advertised and can damage pipes over time. Mechanical methods + enzymatic cleaners work better for most clogs.
**Evidence:** Consumer Reports testing shows many drain cleaners underperform. Plumbers report pipe damage from overuse of chemical cleaners.
**Status:** PLAUSIBLE — evidence supports reduced effectiveness and pipe damage risk, but chemical cleaners do work for some simple clogs.

**What to include:**
- Why Drano often fails (sits on top of clog, doesn't dissolve it)
- When chemical cleaners make sense (minor, surface-level clogs only)
- The proper plunging technique (cover overflow hole first)
- Shop vac method for stubborn clogs (suck out vs. push through)
- When to call a plumber with a camera (persistent clogs = possible pipe damage)
