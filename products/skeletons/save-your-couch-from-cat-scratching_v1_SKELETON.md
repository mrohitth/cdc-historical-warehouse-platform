## PROBLEM DIAGNOSIS

**Core Problem:** Cat owners face furniture destruction from scratching behavior, try piecemeal solutions that fail, and face mounting pressure (from partners, roommates, or themselves) to consider declawing — a procedure they sense is wrong but don't have a framework to avoid.

**Root Cause Cluster:**
1. **Wrong scratching surface** — cat doesn't have an acceptable alternative that matches the appeal of sofa fabric (texture, stability, location)
2. **Stress/territorial signalling** — scratching intensifies when environment feels uncertain (new furniture, new people, other pets)
3. **Under-stimulation** — scratching doubles as displacement activity when the cat has insufficient play, climbing, or hunting outlets
4. **Misread signals** — owners punish scratching without addressing why the cat is targeting fabric specifically

**Who This Is For:** Cat owners with furniture-scratching cats who want lasting, humane solutions. Not for medical scratching (fleas, allergies). Not for declawing advocates.

## SOLUTION PILLARS

### Pillar 1: The Right Scratching Post Formula (Verified)
**Claim:** Most scratching posts fail because they don't match what makes furniture appealing: stability, texture, and placement. Matching those three factors dramatically increases success.
**Evidence:** Frontiers in Veterinary Science (2024) studied factors affecting undesired scratching — personality, environment, and activity level all influence scratching intensity. Scent, texture, and stability are primary attractors for cats.
**Status:** VERIFIED — peer-reviewed 2024 study + multiple behaviorist sources.

**What to include:**
- Why your current scratching post probably fails (too wobbly, wrong material, bad location)
- The 3-factor formula: stability (floor-fixed), texture (sisal/ jute), placement (where the cat already scratches)
- The "double attractor" method: put the post right next to the furniture being scratched, then gradually move it
- Top tested materials ranked by success: sisal rope > cardboard > jute > carpet

### Pillar 2: The Environmental Swap (Verified)
**Claim:** Covering the targeted furniture temporarily with a less-scratching-preferred material breaks the habit loop without confrontation.
**Evidence:** Double-sided tape (Cat西斯), plastic floor mats, vinyl runner (texture cats avoid), or furniture covers make the target unappealing while the cat re-trains onto the post.
**Status:** VERIFIED — widely used by behaviorists and in trap-neuter-return/ shelter environments.

**What to include:**
- Step-by-step: what to cover with and how to apply it
- Duration: minimum 3-4 weeks before removing (cat needs to fully transfer habit)
- How to remove the cover gradually without triggering regression
- Alternatives: plastic runner nub-side up, vinyl sheeting, aluminum foil (temporary)

### Pillar 3: Nail Caps as the Humane Middle Ground (Verified)
**Claim:** Soft nail caps (Brand: Soft Claws, Docs) glue onto cat's claws and render furniture damage impossible during the retraining period. They are not permanent and do not affect the cat's ability to scratch.
**Evidence:** PetMD, ASPCA, and multiple veterinary behaviorists recommend nail caps as a non-surgical alternative to declawing in the US and Europe.
**Status:** VERIFIED — widely endorsed by veterinarians and behaviorists.

**What to include:**
- Why they work: the scratching is still happening, just no damage results
- Application method: 5-step process for applying at home
- How long they last: 4-6 weeks per application
- What to say to skeptical partners/family ("It's like a manicure for cats, they don't feel it")
- When to avoid: infected paws, senior cats with mobility issues
