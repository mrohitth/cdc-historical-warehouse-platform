## PROBLEM DIAGNOSIS

**Core Problem:** First-time homeowners receive no training. They inherit a property and suddenly face a running list of tasks — seasonal chores, preventive maintenance, repairs — with zero context for what matters, what can wait, and what will cost thousands if ignored. Generic checklists exist but assume baseline knowledge they don't have.

**Root Cause Cluster:**
1. **Information overload without priority** — existing checklists list 200+ tasks with no sense of urgency, cost, or consequence for skipping
2. **No cost context** — homeowners don't know if something is a $20 DIY fix or a $5,000 contractor emergency
3. **No DIY vs. professional decision framework** — they freeze on every decision
4. **No tracking system** — they do things once and have no idea when to do them again

**Who This Is For:** New homeowners in their first 1-3 years who feel overwhelmed by the scope of home maintenance. They've searched Reddit, found generic checklists, and still don't know where to start. They want one clear guide that tells them what matters most.

## SOLUTION PILLARS

### Pillar 1: The Seasonal Priority System (Verified)
**Claim:** Home maintenance tasks follow a seasonal pattern. Focusing on the right tasks at the right time prevents most expensive emergency repairs.
**Evidence:** BHG, Zillow, and Fannie Mae all publish seasonal maintenance frameworks confirming this approach is the industry standard for homeowner education.
**Status:** VERIFIED — well-established in home maintenance best practices.

**What to include:**
- Spring: HVAC tune-up, gutter deep clean, roof inspection, window cleaning, dryer vent cleaning
- Summer: exterior paint/trim check, irrigation audit, septic or sewer scope, garage door hardware
- Fall: gutter cleanup, furnace priming, weatherstripping, pipe winterization, smoke/CO detector test
- Winter: frozen pipe prevention, ice dam monitoring, furnace filter changes, water heater flush
- The 5 tasks that never wait (regardless of season): smoke detectors, water heater T&P valve, main water shut-off location, sewer cleanout location, circuit breaker map

### Pillar 2: The Cost-Context Decision Tree (Verified)
**Claim:** Homeowners can make better maintenance decisions when they know typical cost ranges and the consequence of postponing.
**Evidence:** HVAC maintenance costs verified at $75-500/year for annual contracts (EcoWatch, Armadillo, John Owens Services, 2024-2025). Gutter cleaning: $150-300 pro / DIY free. These are widely published figures.
**Status:** VERIFIED — cost data from multiple current sources.

**What to include:**
- Task cost ranges: DIY cost vs. contractor range for 20 most common tasks
- Decision tree: Can I do this myself? Should I do it now? What happens if I wait 6 months? 2 years?
- The "$500 rule" — when a repair costs more than $500 to fix, get 3 written quotes
- Emergency vs. urgent vs. routine — how to categorize any incoming repair need
- Most expensive first-year surprises: HVAC repair avg $150-500, water heater replacement $800-2,000, foundation issues $4,000-15,000+ — and how to catch them early

### Pillar 3: The Home Maintenance Tracking System (Verified)
**Claim:** A simple one-page annual tracking system prevents tasks from falling through the cracks and builds the homeowner's knowledge over time.
**Evidence:** CrossCountry Mortgage, Portland Real Estate, and other homeowner resource sites recommend annual tracking systems.
**Status:** VERIFIED — standard practice in homeowner education.

**What to include:**
- The Annual Maintenance Calendar: one-page visual showing which months to do which tasks
- The "Home Notebook" system: where to keep receipts, contractor invoices, appliance manuals, warranty info
- How to build a property profile: age of roof, HVAC, water heater, major appliances — all in one place
- Seasonal "walk-around" checklist: 15-minute quarterly inspection of the exterior and key systems
- What to photograph and document (and where to store it)
