## PROBLEM DIAGNOSIS

**Core Problem:** A cat repeatedly licks, bites, or scratches at the same spot on its body — pulling fur out, creating bald patches, and eventually breaking the skin until it's raw, scabbed, or bleeding. The owner has tried steroids, antibiotics, allergy medications, and dietary changes. The vet says nothing is medically wrong. The cat won't stop. The owner feels helpless watching their pet hurt itself, and the standard cone-and-steroids approach isn't working.

**Root Cause Cluster:**
1. **Medical causes are common** — In one veterinary study, 76% of cats presenting with presumed psychogenic alopecia had an identifiable medical cause (demodicosis, cutaneous lymphoma, food allergies, atopy). True psychogenic alopecia is a diagnosis of exclusion.
2. **Pain as a trigger** — GI pain (IBD), dental disease, arthritis, and urinary issues can all manifest as compulsive grooming at a specific body site (the leg, the flank). The cat is responding to referred pain.
3. **The endorphin loop** — Excessive grooming releases endorphins. The cat is caught in a self-reinforcing cycle: anxiety → grooming → temporary relief → more grooming. Breaking this loop requires addressing both the anxiety and the physical habit.
4. **Owner frustration loop** — E-collars and cones cause stress. Medications have side effects. Owners are overwhelmed by conflicting advice.

**Who This Is For:** Cat owners whose cats have been licking themselves raw, bloody, or bald for weeks or months — and for whom standard allergy/steroid treatment hasn't worked. They are frustrated, worried, and don't know what to try next.

## SOLUTION PILLARS

### Pillar 1: Rule Out Medical Causes — Properly
**Fact Check: VERIFIED**

The most important step is a systematic medical workup. Many owners have been to the vet once, tried steroids, and been told "it's behavioral." That may be premature:
- **Urinalysis and culture** — rules out UTI and FLUTD (lower urinary tract disease), which causes referred pain to the hind legs and groin
- **Bloodwork + thyroid panel** — hyperthyroidism causes increased grooming; early kidney disease causes pruritus
- **Full dermatologic workup** — skin scrapings, fungal culture (ringworm), ectoparasite treatment trial
- **Dietary elimination trial** — 8-12 weeks on a novel or hydrolyzed protein diet; food allergy is a common overlooked cause
- **Dental exam under sedation** — periodontal disease, FORLs, and tooth resorption cause mouth and jaw pain that manifests as face/forepaw grooming
- **Abdominal ultrasound** — if GI signs are present (vomiting, diarrhea, weight loss); IBD can cause pain referred to the flank/groin area

**The critical insight:** In a 21-cat veterinary study, 76% had an identifiable medical cause. Only 10% were pure psychogenic alopecia. A "nothing wrong with the bloodwork" statement is not a complete workup.

### Pillar 2: Pharmacological Options When Steroids Have Failed
**Fact Check: VERIFIED (for drug categories); PLAUSIBLE (for specific claims)**

When behavioral/anxiety is confirmed or strongly suspected:
- **Gabapentin (10mg/kg BID-TID)** — First-line anxiolytic for feline anxiety-based grooming. Widely used in veterinary behavior. Sedation is the main side effect. Often combined with other interventions.
- **Clonazepam (0.5-1mg/cat BID)** — Benzodiazepine with anxiolytic and muscle relaxant properties. Reduces the anxiety component of the grooming cycle. Risk of sedation and dependency with long-term use.
- **Fluoxetine/Prozac (1-2mg/kg daily)** — SSRI used for severe anxiety-driven self-trauma. Takes 4-6 weeks for full effect. Not a quick fix.
- **Atopica (cyclosporine)** — Used for atopic dermatitis but also has behavioral applications. Expensive but effective for some cats.

**Anti-anxiety supplements:**
- **Zylkene (alpha-casozepine)** — Milk protein-derived calming aid. Low side effect profile. Mild to moderate efficacy.
- **Solliquin (l-theanine, phycocyanin)** — Veterinary-calming supplement. Evidence base is emerging.

**Physical barriers (while medication takes effect):**
- **Inflatable e-collar** — Less stressful than traditional plastic cones. Allows visibility and movement while preventing access.
- **Recovery suit / cat bodysuit** — Fabric suit that covers the body. Lets skin heal while cat remains comfortable.
- **Mitten socks / paw covers** — For cats specifically targeting their paws.

### Pillar 3: Environmental Enrichment and Anxiety Reduction
**Fact Check: VERIFIED**

Anxiety is the fuel for the grooming loop. Reducing baseline anxiety reduces the urge:
- **Hunt-Catch-Kill-Eat cycle** — 2-3 structured play sessions per day with wand toys. The predatory sequence must be completed for stress relief.
- **Food puzzles** — Replace or supplement bowl feeding. Working for food reduces generalized anxiety.
- **Vertical space** — Cat trees, shelving, window perches. Increases sense of territory and security, especially in multi-cat homes.
- **Feliway Classic (pheromone diffuser)** — Feliway mimics feline facial pheromones and has evidence for reducing stress-related behaviors in multi-cat households.
- **Multi-cat household assessment** — If there is tension between cats (staring, chasing, blocking), this is a direct anxiety source. Separate resources (litter boxes, feeding stations, resting spots) reduce conflict.
- **Routine and predictability** — Cats with anxiety thrive on consistent feeding times, play times, and environmental routines. Disruption (new baby, move, construction noise) can trigger grooming.
