## PROBLEM DIAGNOSIS

**Core Problem:** Parents face daily morning warfare getting children out of bed, dressed, fed, and to school on time. What should be a 30-minute routine stretches to 90 minutes of nagging, yelling, and stress. Parents arrive at work already drained. The root issue is that mornings are managed reactively rather than with a reliable system, and children have no ownership over the process.

**Root Cause Cluster:**
1. **No前一晚prep** — Mornings are chaotic because tasks that could be done the night before (backpack, clothes, lunches) are deferred to morning.
2. **No visual routine** — Children don't know what comes next or how much time each task takes. The parent is the internal narration, not a visible system.
3. **Wake-up mismatch** — Children's circadian rhythms make early wake-ups genuinely difficult, especially in winter darkness. Asking a night-owl child to function at 6:30am is biologically unfair.
4. **No accountability structure** — The parent enforces everything. When the parent stops hovering, the routine collapses. Kids need a system that works without parent-as-drill sergeant.
5. **Screen interference** — Many children use devices first thing in the morning, creating a stimulation mismatch before school.

**Who This Is For:** Parents of school-age children (ages 5-12) who experience daily morning battles. Especially relevant for working parents who need to leave by a fixed time and for households with multiple children in different schools.

### Pillar 2: Visual Routine and Time Awareness (Verified)
**Claim:** Children who can see their morning routine on a chart complete tasks without being reminded, reducing parent-nagging load and building kid independence.
**Evidence:** Visual schedules are standard practice in early childhood education and widely used for children with ADHD. Research on task initiation in children shows that external visual cues significantly improve compliance and reduce need for verbal reminders.
**Status:** VERIFIED — visual schedules are evidence-based intervention for routine building.

**What to include:**
- The 6-step visual morning chart (wake up → get dressed → eat breakfast → brush teeth → pack up → shoes on)
- How to create the chart with your child (buy-in matters)
- Time-based version for older kids (clock-reading accountability)
- The "you're on your own" milestone: when to let kids run their own morning
- What to do when kids deliberately ignore the chart

### Pillar 3: Wake-Up Light and Winter Morning Solutions (Plausible)
**Claim:** Wake-up lights that simulate sunrise eliminate the jarring alarm-and-darkness dynamic that makes winter mornings especially brutal for children.
**Evidence:** Light therapy for seasonal morning difficulty is well-established. Wake-up lights are commercial products with positive user reviews, but controlled studies specifically in children are limited. The mechanism (light signaling circadian rhythm) is scientifically grounded.
**Status:** PLAUSIBLE — light therapy mechanism is verified; specific wake-up light product efficacy is consumer-reported. Recommend as option, not required.

**What to include:**
- Wake-up light recommendations (Philips Hue sunrise, Lumie, budget alternatives)
- How to implement gradual earlier wake times (15 min earlier every 3 days)
- The 30-minute before-school calm zone: no screens, low stimulation
- Breakfast options that actually stick: protein-rich meals that fuel focus
- What to do on "zero morning" days when everything goes wrong
