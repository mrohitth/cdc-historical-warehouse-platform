# 3. PRODUCT ARCHITECTURE

### PART 1: THE DIAGNOSIS

*Understand exactly what's causing your flooding*

**Chapter 1: Where Is the Water Coming From?**

The reader will: Distinguish between three flooding types — surface water intrusion (foundation cracks, window wells), groundwater seepage (high water table), and sewer backup (combined sewer overflow through floor drains). They will use a visual decision tree and a 10-question "Flood Type Assessment" to identify their specific problem. Sewer backup through floor drains during heavy rain is the target problem for this guide.

**Chapter 2: Is Your Home in the Risk Zone?**

The reader will: Determine whether their home uses a combined sewer system (CSS) and whether their area has documented overflow events. They will find their municipality's sewer infrastructure maps and identify if their block has known backup history. Key fact: combined sewer systems serve roughly 40% of U.S. sewer infrastructure, primarily in older cities.

**Chapter 3: The Floor Drain Failure Mode**

The reader will: Understand why floor drains fail during heavy rain — when municipal systems overload, pressure reverses and water backs up through the lowest opening in the home (the floor drain). They will understand the critical role of the "trap" and why standard drain traps cannot prevent backup under pressure. This chapter establishes why DIY solutions (plungers, drain covers) are insufficient for sewer backup scenarios.

---

### PART 2: THE SOLUTIONS

*Ranked by effectiveness, budget, and installation complexity*

**Chapter 4: Backwater Valve — The Primary Defense**

The reader will: Understand what a backwater valve is (a one-way valve that allows water to flow out but prevents backup from entering), the two main types (check valve and gate valve), and the installation requirements. They will understand that backwater valves must be installed at the point where the home's drain line exits the foundation — requiring excavation or interior installation depending on configuration.

*Fact-Check*: Backwater valve installation costs $300-$800 typically. Some municipalities offer subsidies up to $1,250 (e.g., Toronto). **VERIFIED**

**Chapter 5: Sump Pump System — The Water Removal Engine**

The reader will: Understand how a sump pump works (collects water in a basin and pumps it away from the foundation), the difference between submersible and pedestal pumps, and why a functioning sump pump is critical even if a backwater valve is installed. They will understand that sump pumps require both primary (electric) and battery backup systems for full protection.

*Fact-Check*: Sump pump installation $500-$1,200. Battery backup systems add $200-$500. **VERIFIED**

**Chapter 6: The Battery Backup — Non-Negotiable**

The reader will: Understand why the worst time for a sump pump to fail is during a storm (when power outages commonly occur), and why a battery backup is not optional for full flood protection. They will evaluate three backup options: battery-powered backup pump, water-powered backup (for homes with city water pressure), and generator integration. They will understand runtime expectations and battery life.

**Chapter 7: Water Alarms and Smart Monitoring**

The reader will: Implement a layered early warning system using water leak detectors ($20-$50 each) placed near the water heater, washer, furnace, and floor drains. They will understand how smart home integration (water sensors that send phone alerts) provides critical response time during off-hours flooding. This is the "last line of defense" for early detection when no other system is yet installed.

### PART 3: THE IMPLEMENTATION ROADMAP

*What to do first, second, and third based on your budget*

**Chapter 8: Tiered Protection Levels**

The reader will: Assess their current protection level (from "nothing" to "fully protected") and create a ranked implementation plan. Three tiers:
- Tier 1 (Essential — $50-$200): Water alarms,抬高 furniture and valuables, clear floor drains of debris, check sump pump operation monthly.
- Tier 2 (Standard — $500-$1,500): Battery backup for existing sump pump, or new sump pump installation if none exists. Consider interior backwater valve if exterior installation is not feasible.
- Tier 3 (Comprehensive — $1,500-$3,000): Full backwater valve installation, new sump pump with battery backup, exterior drainage improvements, smart monitoring integration.

**Chapter 9: What to Look for in a Contractor**

The reader will: Avoid common contractor pitfalls (no permit, improper valve sizing, improper installation angle). They will understand what questions to ask — specifically about permits, valve location, and warranty. They will understand that plumbing contractors (not general handymen) are the appropriate trade for backwater valve installation.

### PART 4: THE MAINTENANCE AND LONG-TERM

*Sustainability, testing, and peace of mind*

**Chapter 10: The Monthly Test Protocol**

The reader will: Implement a simple monthly sump pump test (pour a bucket of water, verify pump activates and empties basin). They will understand how to test their backwater valve (there should be a cleanout access point) and when to call a plumber for inspection. They will set calendar reminders for seasonal tasks (battery check in spring before heavy rain season, etc.).

**Chapter 11: When It's Already Flooding — Damage Control**

The reader will: If flooding occurs despite prevention measures, execute an immediate damage control sequence: shut off electricity to affected area, document damage with photos, contact insurance, begin drying process within 24-48 hours to prevent mold. This chapter exists for worst-case scenarios and is not a substitute for prevention.
