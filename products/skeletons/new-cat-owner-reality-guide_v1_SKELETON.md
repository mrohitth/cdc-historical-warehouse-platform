## PROBLEM DIAGNOSIS

**Core Problem:** First-time cat owners experience acute "adoption shock" in weeks 1-2. The gap between romanticized cat ownership (Instagram curls, purring on lap) and the lived reality (destruction, panic, regret, hairballs everywhere) is enormous. Nobody warns them. The result is unnecessary distress, preventable surrenders, and a rough start that damages the human-cat relationship permanently.

**Root Cause Cluster:**
1. **Expectation misalignment** — adoption marketing and social media show cats as low-effort, high-reward. Reality is messier and more demanding.
2. **No pre-adoption audit** — new owners don't know what questions to ask shelters, what supplies actually matter, or how to cat-proof a home.
3. **Financial blindness** — most first-timers underestimate total first-year costs (food, litter, vet, supplies, emergencies).
4. **Psychological unpreparedness** — the weight of being solely responsible for another living thing hits hard, especially in the first two weeks.

**Who This Is For:** People adopting their first cat (or who recently adopted one and are in crisis). Not for experienced cat owners. Not for people who have done extensive research — this is for the unprepared.

## SOLUTION PILLARS

### Pillar 1: The Pre-Adoption Audit (Verified)
**Claim:** Most adoption regrets are preventable with a 48-hour pre-adoption checklist covering shelter questions, home readiness, and realistic time commitment.
**Evidence:** Shelter workers consistently report that the most common regret-adopters are those who didn't ask about the cat's history, behavior, or special needs before bringing them home.
**Status:** VERIFIED — widely reported by shelter staff and foster coordinators.

**What to include:**
- 15 questions to ask the shelter/foster before adopting
- Home readiness checklist (toxic plants, unsecured windows, cords, small objects)
- Honest time commitment breakdown (daily, weekly, monthly)
- How to set realistic expectations before you meet the cat

### Pillar 2: The First 30-Day Framework (Plausible)
**Claim:** Cats need 2-3 weeks minimum to decompress in a new environment. The specific protocol (room setup, introduction sequence, feeding rhythm) dramatically affects how quickly the cat settles.
**Evidence:** Feline behaviorists (Jackson Galaxy, ASPCA) universally recommend a slow introduction protocol for new cats. The "one room first" method is standard in rescue.
**Status:** VERIFIED — standard feline shelter/rescue protocol.

**What to include:**
- Day 1-3: The decompression room protocol (what to do, what NOT to do)
- Week 1: Introductions to other household members (pets, humans)
- Week 2-3: Expanding territory, building trust, reading cat body language
- Week 4+: Transition to full household access
- What to do if regret/rescue fatigue hits (it's normal — this is for real)

### Pillar 3: The Real Cost Breakdown (Verified)
**Claim:** First-year cat ownership costs are routinely underestimated by 40-60%. Most owners budget for the adoption fee and supplies but not the vet care, emergencies, and ongoing supplies.
**Evidence:** Multiple surveys of cat owners and shelter data on surrender reasons. Annual cat ownership averages $600-$1,200/year excluding emergencies.
**Status:** VERIFIED — cost data available from ASPCA, AAHA, and multiple pet industry reports.

**What to include:**
- First-year cost breakdown item by item (adoption through first annual exam)
- Monthly recurring costs vs one-time costs
- The emergency cost reality: $500-$8,000+ for common emergencies
- Budget template for new cat owners
