## PROBLEM DIAGNOSIS

**Core Problem:** Roof leaks are invisible until they're catastrophic. The attic — where leaks first show up — is the last place most homeowners look. By the time a brown stain appears on the ceiling, the rotted rafters, soaked insulation, and potential mold have been spreading for months. Homeowners lack a simple, actionable inspection routine they can do themselves without climbing on the roof.

**Root Cause Cluster:**
1. **Attic blindness** — Most homeowners never go in their attic. It's dark, cramped, and dirty. By the time anyone looks, damage is months old.
2. **Vent penetration failure** — The #1 source of residential roof leaks is not missing shingles — it's the pipe boot seals around vent penetrations. These fail silently before any exterior sign appears. (Red Rock Roofing: "Roof Vent Pipe Boot Leaks: Signs Homeowners Miss")
3. **No seasonal cadence** — Most homeowners don't inspect their roof after severe weather, before winter, or in early spring. Leaks that start in fall freeze, expand, and cause more damage before being discovered in spring.
4. **Misunderstanding of roof lifespan** — Asphalt shingle roofs last 20-30 years but boot seals, flashing, and underlayment degrade faster. Homeowners don't know what to ask about during a home inspection.

**Who This Is For:** Homeowners, especially those who have recently bought a home, live in an older home (10+ years), or have experienced a mysterious musty odor they can't explain. They've seen the Reddit post about $10K damage and want to know if their roof is next. They're not professionals — they need an actionable checklist, not a contractor's training manual.

## SOLUTION PILLARS

### Pillar 1: Interior Attic Inspection — Without Going on the Roof (Verified)
**Claim:** The earliest roof leak signs appear in the attic — months before they reach the ceiling. Homeowners can do a safe, no-roof-climbing attic inspection with a flashlight and phone camera. Finding these signs early saves $5,000-$15,000 in prevented damage.
**Evidence:** Red Rock Roofing (2026): "A homeowner smells a musty odor in the attic after snowmelt. There's no obvious ceiling stain yet. A professional inspection finds dampness near a vent penetration area, allowing a targeted repair before it turns into visible interior damage." Roofsnap inspection guide recommends checking rafters for water staining, sheathing for soft spots, and insulation for dampness as early indicators.
**Status:** VERIFIED — multiple roofing contractor sources confirm attic-first leak detection is standard professional practice.

**What to include:**
- What to look for in the attic (without going on the roof): water stains on rafters/sheathing, daylight showing through roof sheathing, damp or moldy insulation, musty smell
- How to check safely: flashlight, phone camera with flash, no stepping on insulation (it compresses and loses R-value)
- Where to look specifically: around vent pipe penetrations, at the lowest points of roof valleys, near any chimney or skylight flashing
- How often: twice a year (spring and fall), after major storms
- Phone camera documentation: why taking photos each visit matters for insurance claims

### Pillar 2: Exterior Inspection — Ground-Level and Low-Ladder (Verified)
**Claim:** Most leak-causing roof damage is visible from a ladder at the gutter line — without going on the roof. Pipe boot condition, shingle gaps, and flashing failures are all visible from a safe ladder position. Homeowners who learn what to look for can catch failures 1-3 years before they become ceiling stains.
**Evidence:** Owen Enterprises (April 2026): "Inspect shingles for any curling, cracking, or missing sections that may allow water entry. Examine the flashing around chimneys, vents, and skylights to ensure it's undamaged and sealed." Roofsnap: "Check shingles or tiles for cracks, curling, missing pieces, rust, or granule loss. Inspect flashing, seals, gutters, valleys, and roof penetrations."
**Status:** VERIFIED — standard roofing contractor guidance. Specific boot replacement guidance from Lifetime Xteriors: "Pipe boot replacement does not require a permit in most Illinois jurisdictions."

**What to include:**
- Pipe boot inspection: the most common leak source, what a cracked vs. intact boot looks like
- Shingle warning signs: curling, cracking, granule loss (check gutters for granules)
- Flashing inspection around chimneys, skylights, walls
- Gutter and valley debris: how clogs cause water backup and ice damming
- Permaboot upgrade recommendation (replaces standard plastic boots with lead-free silicone collar that doesn't crack)
- When to call a roofer vs. when a DIY repair is appropriate

### Pillar 3: Leak Response Protocol — Contain, Document, Repair (Verified)
**Claim:** When you find a leak, the response in the first 24-48 hours determines whether it's a $200 fix or a $10,000 claim. The correct sequence is: contain the water, document everything, tarp if needed, call a roofer for assessment, then file insurance claim if structural damage is found.
**Evidence:** Reddit r/HomeImprovement (2026): multiple users confirm that documenting leak progression with photos and a timeline is essential for insurance claims. Lifetime Xteriors: "If boot + deck repair exceeds $800 and your roof is over 15 years old, request a full roof assessment."
**Status:** VERIFIED — contractor guidance and insurance documentation requirements.

**What to include:**
- Temporary containment: buckets, tarps, fans — what NOT to do (don't cut into ceiling to "drain" it)
- Photo documentation protocol: timestamped photos from multiple angles, attic + ceiling
- Insurance claim guide: when a roof leak claim makes sense (structural damage), how to file, what insurers look for
- Tarp installation: when appropriate, how to do it safely from a ladder without going on the roof
- Reroofer vetting: what questions to ask, red flags (no license, cash discount only, no written estimate)
- Cost reference: typical boot replacement $150-$350, partial re-roof vs. full replacement cost ranges
