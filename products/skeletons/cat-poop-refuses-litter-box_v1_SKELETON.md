## 2. EMOTIONAL HOOK

You've cleaned up so many messes you've lost count.

Your cat uses the litter box perfectly — every single time she needs to pee. But when it comes to poop? She waits until you're not looking, then goes on the floor. Right next to the box. Or on the rug. Or the bathroom mat.

You've tried switching litter. You've tried different boxes. You cleaned the existing box more often. Nothing changes.

The frustrating part isn't just the mess — it's that your cat seems to know exactly what she's doing. She uses the box for one thing and not the other. That feels like a choice. Like she's doing it on purpose.

She's not.

When a cat pees in the box but refuses to poop in it, something specific is going on — and it usually falls into one of three categories: a medical issue making defecation painful, a box design that scares her during the act, or a past experience that taught her the box isn't safe for pooping.

This guide walks you through figuring out which one it is, what tests to run, and how to fix it — without surrendering your cat.

## 3. PRODUCT ARCHITECTURE

### Part 1: Medical First — Rule Out the Dangerous Stuff

**Chapter 1: The Constipation Check**
*The reader will be able to:* Identify signs of feline constipation: hard dry stools, straining, crying while pooping, fewer bowel movements than normal (normal = 1-2 per day). Know when constipation is a mild dietary issue vs. a serious blockage. Start a symptom log for the vet.

**Chapter 2: The IBD and Digestive Investigation**
*The reader will be able to:* Recognize signs of inflammatory bowel disease and chronic digestive upset that make pooping painful: vomiting, weight loss, coat deterioration, frequent small stools. Know what tests (blood panel, fecal test, ultrasound) the vet should run to rule this out.

**Chapter 3: Vet Visit Checklist**
*The reader will be able to:* Bring a 3-day poop journal, photo evidence of stool consistency, list of all foods and treats, and observations about when/where the cat poops outside the box. Ask the right questions: "Could this be constipation pain?" "Should we test for IBD?" "Is there a structural issue?"

---

### Part 2: Box Design — Why Some Cats Hate Pooping in Certain Boxes

**Chapter 4: The Automatic Litter Box Problem**
*The reader will be able to:* Understand why many cats refuse to poop in automatic litter boxes: the rake mechanism activates while they're mid-defecation, creating a fear response. Know that this is especially common in larger cats and cats who've had bad experiences with the mechanism. Run a diagnostic: has the box cycled while your cat was in it?

**Chapter 5: Hooded Box Aversion**
*The reader will be able to:* Explain why some cats feel trapped in hooded boxes during defecation (defecation is vulnerable — they're exposed in a covered box). Test whether switching to an open box or a top-entry style solves the problem.

**Chapter 6: Box Sizing for Defecation**
*The reader will be able to:* Know that defecation requires more space and a more stable box than urination. Test box dimensions: is the box large enough for the cat to turn around comfortably? Is it stable (noFlex)? Does it have high enough walls to contain the mess?

---

### Part 3: Environmental Causes — The Learned Behavior Problem

**Chapter 7: Poop Anxiety and Past Trauma**
*The reader will be able to:* Identify if the cat had a negative experience in or near the litter box during defecation: pain from constipation, being startled by another pet, punishment after a previous accident. Understand how these experiences create a learned avoidance.

**Chapter 8: Litter Depth and Texture for Defecation**
*The reader will be able to:* Test litter depth (most cats prefer 2 inches of litter to dig and cover) and litter texture (some cats prefer finer sand-like litter for defecation vs. coarser litter for urine). Run a 3-day litter test.

**Chapter 9: Location and Privacy**
*The reader will be able to:* Evaluate box location through the lens of defecation vulnerability: cats need privacy and security when pooping. Identify if a noisy room, a high-traffic area, or proximity to other pets' resources is causing avoidance. Move the box and measure results.

---

### Part 4: The Fix Protocol

**Chapter 10: The 14-Day Poop Retraining Plan**
*The reader will be able to:* Implement a systematic retraining protocol: address medical causes first, switch box type if needed, add a second box with different litter type, track daily progress, and know when the problem is solved vs. when to escalate to a behaviorist.
