## PROBLEM DIAGNOSIS

**Core Problem:** Cat owners are losing sleep — sometimes nightly — because their cat yowls, meows, or cries through the night. They feel guilty for being frustrated, have tried ignoring it without success, and worry their cat is in distress. The meowing doesn't stop until the owner responds or the cat exhausts itself.

**Root Cause Cluster:**
1. **Attention reinforcement loop** — any response (even yelling) teaches the cat that persistence works
2. **Unmet biological needs** — hunting/marking instincts spike at dusk and dawn; indoor cats lack outlet
3. **Undiagnosed medical issues** — hyperthyroidism, cognitive decline, pain, or anxiety driving sudden vocalization changes
4. **Environmental mismatch** — warm house at night triggers active period; cat has no nighttime activity outlet

**Who This Is For:** Cat owners whose cat wakes them repeatedly through the night with loud meowing or yowling. They've tried ignoring it, google searched solutions, maybe tried a few herb packs or calmingplugins. Nothing holds. They're exhausted, frustrated, and worried their cat is suffering.

## SOLUTION PILLARS

### Pillar 1: The Reinforcement Audit (Verified)
**Claim:** Night vocalization is often owner-reinforced. Identifying the exact behavior loop is the first step to breaking it.
**Evidence:** ASPCA and Cornell Feline Health Center confirm attention-seeking vocalization is maintained by owner response — even negative responses like yelling.
**Status:** VERIFIED — feline behavior science consensus.

**What to include:**
- Day-by-day behavior log: what triggers the meowing, what the owner does, what the cat does next
- Why "just ignoring it" often fails — the cat learns extended persistence pays off
- The "zero response protocol": exactly what to do (and not do) during the protest window
- How to协调全家一致 (coordinate all household members)

### Pillar 2: Environmental Night Setup (Verified)
**Claim:** Enriching the nighttime environment reduces active-period vocalization.
**Evidence:** Cornell Feline Health Center, AAFP guidelines: environmental enrichment (feeding schedules, hunting puzzles, territory mapping) reduces stress-driven behavior.
**Status:** VERIFIED — established feline behavioral medicine.

**What to include:**
- Automated feeder with nighttime dispensing (set 30-60 min after typical protest peak)
- Interactive puzzle feeders that extend eating time
- Safe nighttime hiding spots (cats feel safer in high-covered areas at night)
- Night light placement for anxious cats (especially seniors with CDS)
- Why free-feeding worsens food-motivation yowling

### Pillar 3: Medical Triage — When to See a Vet (Verified)
**Claim:** Sudden increases in nighttime vocalization, especially in cats over 7, warrant veterinary screening before behavioral intervention.
**Evidence:** Cornell CDS research, AVMA hyperthyroidism guidelines: increased vocalization is a top sign of hyperthyroidism, cognitive dysfunction, and pain conditions in cats.
**Status:** VERIFIED — clearly established in feline veterinary medicine.

**What to include:**
- The "Vet Visit Checklist" — what to tell the vet, what tests to request (T4 panel, senior chem panel)
- Hyperthyroidism signs: weight loss + increased appetite + vocalization increase
- Cognitive Dysfunction Syndrome (CDS) signs: confusion, meowing at dead ends, staring at walls
- Arthritis in older cats: reluctant to jump, crying during movement changes
- Normal aging vs. concerning changes — how to tell the difference
