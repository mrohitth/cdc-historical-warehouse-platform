## 2. EMOTIONAL HOOK

Every night, you make something different. Every night, you watch your child push the plate away.

You've tried bargaining. "Just three bites." You've tried rewards. "Finish your vegetables and you can have dessert." You've tried tough love. "You don't leave the table until you try it." You've even tried pretending not to care — and that backfired too.

Meanwhile, your mother-in-law suggests your child is "just being stubborn" and your pediatrician mentions "failure to thrive" and your partner wants to "just let her eat what she wants" and you feel like you're failing at the most basic parenting task: feeding your child.

Here's what nobody tells you: **your child's limited diet isn't a reflection of your parenting.** It's not because you gave them too many pouches. It's not because you didn't try hard enough with vegetables first. Selective eating is complex — it involves sensory processing, developmental stages, innate temperament, and sometimes medical factors.

This guide is for you: someone who wants evidence-based strategies, a clear framework, and hope that things can get better. We're going to understand why your child eats what they eat, build a safe-food foundation, and systematically expand from there — without turning mealtimes into war zones.

## 3. PRODUCT ARCHITECTURE

### Part 1: Understanding — Why Your Child Eats What They Eat

**Chapter 1: The Selective Eating Spectrum**
*The reader will be able to:* Identify where their child falls on the selective eating spectrum from "typical picky" to "food neophobia" to "ARFID-risk." Understand what level of support their child needs.

**Chapter 2: Sensory Processing and Food**
*The reader will be able to:* Recognize sensory red flags in their child's eating (textures that cause gagging, colors that trigger refusal, smells that overwhelm). Understand why certain foods feel physically threatening to their child.

**Chapter 3: The 20-Food Inventory**
*The reader will be able to:* Complete a detailed inventory of exactly what their child currently eats — categorized by safe foods, maybe foods, and never foods. Identify patterns in texture, color, temperature, and brand that reveal their child's food profile.

**Chapter 4: When to Involve Your Pediatrician**
*The reader will be able to:* Know which symptoms require medical evaluation: significant weight loss, failure to gain weight, nutritional deficiency symptoms, extreme anxiety around food, physical symptoms (GI issues, constipation). Understand what an occupational therapy feeding evaluation involves.

---

### Part 2: Foundation — Building a Better Mealtime Environment

**Chapter 5: The Division of Responsibility Reframe**
*The reader will be able to:* Apply Ellyn Satter's model: parents decide WHAT and WHEN to eat, children decide IF and HOW MUCH. Stop taking responsibility for your child's food intake — and reclaim your own peace.

**Chapter 6: Pressure-Free Mealtime Setup**
*The reader will be able to:* Create a mealtime environment that reduces anxiety: neutral foods on the table, no forced tasting, no reward/punishment dynamics, sibling behavior management strategies.

**Chapter 7: Safe Food Protocols**
*The reader will be able to:* Ensure at least one "safe food" is available at every meal without making it a reward. Stop catering to the limited diet while simultaneously stopping pressure to expand it.

---

### Part 3: Expansion — Systematic Food Exposure Without Battles

**Chapter 8: The Food Hierarchy System**
*The reader will be able to:* Place foods on a hierarchy from "easily accepted" to "aspirational." Target exposure efforts at the right level — not too easy (boring) and not too hard (overwhelming).

**Chapter 9: The 5-Second Exposure Technique**
*The reader will be able to:* Use non-coercive exposure methods: food modeling, "you don't have to eat it, just see it on the table," repeated casual exposure without expectation. Let familiarity build before requiring tasting.

**Chapter 10: Managing Mealtime Meltdowns**
*The reader will be able to:* Handle food refusal, plate-pushing, and crying without escalating. Have a calm response script ready so they don't react emotionally in the moment.

**Chapter 11: Expanding Texture Tolerance**
*The reader will be able to:* Use a systematic approach to texture progression for children with sensory-based food limitations. Includes specific exercises used by occupational therapists.

---

### Part 4: Maintenance — Long-Term Success and Family Balance

**Chapter 12: Family Meals Without Martyrdom**
*The reader will be able to:* Balance their selective eater's needs with family meals, sibling meals, and restaurant eating. Stop making every meal about the picky eater.

**Chapter 13: Tracking Progress and Celebrating Wins**
*The reader will be able to:* Keep a simple food journal to notice slow gains, know when to push and when to hold steady, and recognize that expansion takes months not weeks.

**Chapter 14: Knowing When to Escalate**
*The reader will be able to:* Recognize when home strategies aren't enough and professional feeding therapy is warranted. Understand what to expect from OT or feeding specialist services.
