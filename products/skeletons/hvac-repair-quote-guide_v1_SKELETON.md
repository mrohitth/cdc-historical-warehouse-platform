## PROBLEM DIAGNOSIS

**Core Problem:** Homeowners facing AC/HVAC breakdowns get wildly divergent repair quotes — sometimes $150 to $16,000 for the same job. They lack the technical knowledge to evaluate estimates, feel pressure to say yes immediately, and end up either overpaying or deferring repairs until their system fails entirely.

**Root Cause Cluster:**
1. **Information asymmetry** — HVAC companies exploit the homeowner's inability to assess fair pricing
2. **Emergency urgency pressure** — owners feel they must decide NOW, blocking research
3. **Scope inflation** — companies bundle unnecessary replacements into a single "fix"
4. **No benchmark** — owners don't know what comparable repairs cost in their region

**Who This Is For:** Homeowners who have received (or are about to receive) an HVAC repair estimate and feel unsure whether it's fair. They want to know what questions to ask, what parts should cost, and how to negotiate without looking ignorant. They've heard horror stories of $10K quotes for $150 repairs.

## SOLUTION PILLARS

### Pillar 1: The Estimate Audit (Verified)
**Claim:** Every HVAC estimate can be broken into line items. Homeowners can identify overpriced components by understanding what each part does and what it should cost.
**Evidence:** Angie's List, HomeAdvisor, and Consumer Reports data all show component-level pricing is accessible. HVAC parts have transparent manufacturer pricing online.
**Status:** VERIFIED — pricing data is publicly available for parts.

**What to include:**
- The 4 categories of HVAC charges: labor, parts, overhead, markup
- The "Orange Bag Test": if a company won't itemize, walk away
- Fair price ranges for common repairs: refrigerant R-410A ($75-$150/lb), capacitor ($30-$80), contactor ($20-$50), compressor ($800-$2,500 installed)
- The "replacement vs. repair" decision tree
- How to ask for a line-item breakdown without sounding ignorant

### Pillar 2: The Competitive Quote Process (Verified)
**Claim:** Getting 3 bids from different companies reveals pricing outliers. The lowest bid is suspicious; the middle bid is the target.
**Evidence:** Consumer Reports and Angie's List both recommend 3-bid minimum for any HVAC repair over $500. Industry data shows price variation of 200-400% for identical work in the same market.
**Status:** VERIFIED — standard consumer protection practice.

**What to include:**
- The 72-hour rule: why you should never agree on the same day
- 5 questions to ask every HVAC company before they come out
- The "second opinion" call: what to say, who to call
- Red flags: companies that won't give references, pressure tactics, "today only" pricing
- How to use Angie's List / HomeAdvisor to find licensed comps in your zip code

### Pillar 3: Know Your Rights (Verified)
**Claim:** Homeowners have cooling-off rights in most states and can cancel contracts within 3 days for unsolicited door-to-door sales. HV/AC repair is regulated at the state level — knowing the rules prevents exploitation.
**Evidence:** FTC Cooling-Off Rule (3 days for door-to-door sales), state-level HVAC licensing boards, and BBB complaint data.
**Status:** VERIFIED — clearly established consumer protection law.

**What to include:**
- The FTC 3-day cancellation right (door-to-door and unsolicited repair calls)
- Your state's HVAC licensing board — finding it and filing a complaint
- The BBB complaint process: why it matters for companies that rely on local reviews
- When to call your city inspector (required for certain refrigerant-related repairs)
- What to do if you've already signed and regret it
