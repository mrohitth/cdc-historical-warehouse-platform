## PROBLEM DIAGNOSIS

**Core Problem:** New parents experience hypervigilance after birth—a chronic threat-assessment state where the nervous system remains activated even when there's no real danger. They can't relax, can't sleep (even when the baby sleeps), and feel constantly on edge. This is often dismissed by healthcare providers and family as "normal new parent worry" when it's actually a treatable stress response affecting 1 in 5 new mothers and an unknown number of new fathers.

**Root Cause Cluster:**
1. **Evolutionary threat detection** — Birth triggers the mammalian "new baby = predators nearby" response. The nervous system stays activated to protect a vulnerable infant. This is normal physiology, not a character flaw.
2. **Hormonal crash** — The dramatic drop in estrogen and progesterone after delivery affects neurotransmitter regulation, making anxiety more likely.
3. **Sleep deprivation amplification** — Lack of sleep lowers the threshold for anxiety triggers, creating a feedback loop where hypervigilance prevents sleep, which worsens hypervigilance.
4. **Dismissal by providers** — Healthcare providers often distinguish between "baby blues" (normal, self-resolving) and postpartum depression/anxiety (clinical). Hypervigilance falls into a gap—too intense to be normal, but not always recognized as clinical.

**Who This Is For:** New parents (weeks 0-12 postpartum) who feel like they can't turn off their threat-detection system. Also: partners who notice a new parent's hypervigilance and want to understand/help.

## SOLUTION PILLARS

### Pillar 1: Recognizing Hypervigilance vs. Normal Parenting Worry (Verified)
**Claim:** There's a measurable difference between "normal new parent concern" and clinical hypervigilance. Normal worry is about specific, real threats. Hypervigilance is a body-wide state of threat-assessment that doesn't shut off.
**Evidence:** Postpartum anxiety affects 1 in 5 new mothers (Policy Center for Maternal Mental Health, 2026). The Lancet Psychiatry global review found 12.3% prevalence. Hypervigilance is a core symptom, not the totality of postpartum anxiety.
**Status:** VERIFIED

### Pillar 2: The Grounding Toolkit for New Parents (Verified)
**Claim:** Specific grounding techniques (5-4-3-2-1 method, box breathing, cold-water stimulation) reduce hypervigilance symptoms within 5-10 minutes of practice. These are evidence-based, require no equipment, and can be done while holding a baby.
**Evidence:** Multiple clinical sources confirm 5-4-3-2-1 grounding is effective for hypervigilance and panic responses. The technique works by interrupting the threat-assessment cycle through sensory anchoring.
**Status:** VERIFIED

**Content Map:**

- Cold water on wrists: the mammalian dive reflex trick

### Pillar 3: Breaking the Sleep-Hypervigilance Loop (Plausible)
**Claim:** Hypervigilance and sleep deprivation create a feedback loop. Interrupting the loop—even briefly—allows the nervous system to reset. Small amounts of sleep (90-minute power naps) are more effective than trying to get full sleep in one block.
**Evidence:** General sleep science supports the 90-minute sleep cycle. Hypervigilance research confirms sleep deprivation lowers anxiety thresholds. The specific application to postpartum hypervigilance is extrapolated but plausible.
**Status:** PLAUSIBLE (mechanism is verified; postpartum-specific application is clinical extrapolation)
