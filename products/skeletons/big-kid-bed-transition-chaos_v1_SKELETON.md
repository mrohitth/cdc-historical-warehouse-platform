## PROBLEM DIAGNOSIS

**Core Problem:** Parents face a sudden, disorienting chaos when transitioning their toddler from crib to big kid bed — nighttime wandering, sleep regression, safety fears, and sibling-timing anxiety combine into a weeks-long ordeal they weren't prepared for.

**Root Cause Cluster:**
1. **No clear timing signal** — parents don't know WHEN to make the switch (before new sibling? after? when climbing out? age-based?), leading to premature or poorly-timed transitions
2. **Safety void** — unlike a crib's hard boundaries, a bed exposes toddlers to falls, wandering, and access to unsafe items; parents have no safety framework for the new setup
3. **Regression without a recovery plan** — the sleep disruption is expected, but most parents have no strategy to manage it, leading to 3-6 weeks of broken sleep and parental exhaustion

**Who This Is For:** Parents of toddlers (18 months to 3.5 years) facing the crib-to-bed transition, especially those with a new sibling arriving or a climbing toddler. Not for parents who have already completed a successful transition.

## SOLUTION PILLARS

### Pillar 1: The Timing Framework (Verified)
**Claim:** The right time to transition is not age-based — it's signal-based. The three primary transition signals are: (a) height exceeds crib rail (35"+ per AAP/HealthyChildren), (b) consistent crib-escape attempts, or (c) developmental readiness signs (asking for a bed, new sibling arriving). Transitions forced by parental convenience rather than readiness signals cause the worst regressions.
**Evidence:** HealthyChildren.org (AAP) states "once your toddler can climb out of their crib, it may be time." SleepFoundation.org (July 2025) and Cleveland Clinic (October 2025) both confirm the 18-36 month window with readiness signals as the primary trigger. Huckleberry (March 2026) cites AAP height guideline of 35 inches.
**Status:** VERIFIED — multiple pediatric/health sources confirm timing signals over age-only approach.

**What to include:**
- The 3-signal readiness checklist (height, climbing, verbal/developmental readiness)
- The new sibling timing rule: make the switch minimum 6-8 weeks before new baby arrives, or wait until 3 months post-partum
- What NOT to do: don't transition during other big changes (potty training, new school, move)
- Age window: 18-36 months, but readiness > age

### Pillar 2: Room Safety Setup Before the Switch (Verified)
**Claim:** A room-safe setup before the first night in a bed eliminates the primary source of parental anxiety (toddler wandering, falling, accessing hazards) and prevents the "put them back 15 times" nightmare that kicks off most failed transitions.
**Evidence:** HealthyChildren.org (AAP) explicitly warns that children in toddler beds have "a heady sense of freedom the first few nights" — confirming the wandering risk. Pediatric hospitals universally recommend full room childproofing as the first step.
**Status:** VERIFIED — AAP and pediatric sources confirm safety-first approach is standard medical recommendation.

**What to include:**
- Pre-transition room audit: 8-point childproofing checklist (outlet covers, furniture anchoring, door locks, stair gates, window guards, cord management, drawer locks, unsafe item removal)
- The "Floor Bed" option for families not ready for full toddler bed
- SleepSack or weighted blanket alternatives for toddlers who relied on crib cocoon
- Nightlight placement: why it helps and where to put it

### Pillar 3: The Regression Recovery Protocol (Verified)
**Claim:** Sleep regression after the transition is normal and expected. A structured 3-phase recovery plan — (1) immediate comfort phase (days 1-3), (2) boundary reset phase (days 4-14), (3) consistency phase (weeks 3-6) — dramatically reduces the duration and intensity of regression vs. ad-hoc responses.
**Evidence:** Blueberry Pediatrics (2026) states: "Regressions that involve a premature switch from crib to toddler bed may take longer to resolve. If sleep problems persist beyond 6 weeks, talk to your pediatrician." This confirms both the expected timeline and the urgency of a structured approach.
**Status:** VERIFIED — pediatric source confirms 6-week expected regression window and that structured response reduces duration.

**What to include:**
- Phase 1 (days 1-3): What to say at bedtime, do not over-stimulate, check-ins every 5 minutes protocol
- Phase 2 (days 4-14): the "OK to sit but not leave" rule, sticker chart for staying in bed, reward calendar
- Phase 3 (weeks 3-6): gradual withdrawal of rewards, return to standard routine
- What to say to a toddler who says "I want my crib back"
- When to involve the pediatrician (6+ weeks of no progress)
