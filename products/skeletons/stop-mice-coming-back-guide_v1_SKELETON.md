## PROBLEM DIAGNOSIS

**Core Problem:** Homeowners pay for exterminators ($200-$1,500 per visit) or set basic traps, only to find new mouse activity weeks or months later. The real issue isn't the mice inside — it's the entry points they keep using that standard exterminator visits don't address. The homeowner never learned where the mice actually came from, so the cycle repeats endlessly.

**Root Cause Cluster:**
1. **You never found all the entry points** — mice need only 1/4 inch (about the size of a dime) to get inside. Homeowners spot one obvious gap, seal it, and miss six others.
2. **Traps without sealing is a losing game** — killing mice that are already inside does nothing if new ones keep arriving through unsealed openings the same night.
3. **Exterminators treat, they don't seal** — most standard pest control visits are spray-and-leave. They kill current mice but leave the highway open for the next ones.
4. **Wrong inspection method** — looking for obvious holes, not understanding where mice actually enter (utility line penetrations, gaps behind appliances, foundation cracks, garage door edges).

**Who This Is For:** Homeowners who have a recurring mouse problem and are tired of the cycle. They've tried traps, maybe called an exterminator once or twice, and the mice keep coming back. They want to solve this permanently without spending $1,000+ on repeated professional visits.

**Differentiation from existing catalog:** Existing home maintenance guides cover plumbing, roofing, HVAC, and ants. No current product addresses the specific recurring-mouse problem through an entry-point-first methodology. The ant control guide covers ant ID + sealing but uses a different pest type and treatment protocol.

## SOLUTION PILLARS

### Pillar 1: The 1/4-Inch Rule + Systematic Entry Point Audit (Verified)
**Claim:** A mouse can squeeze through any opening the size of a dime (1/4 inch). Your job is to find every gap that size or larger around the entire exterior of your home — not just the obvious ones.
**Evidence:** PCI Pest Control (2026), Xcluder (2026), and EcoGuard Pest Management all state mice can fit through 1/4-inch openings. Common missed locations include gaps around utility lines where they penetrate foundations, spaces where garage walls meet concrete floors, HVAC duct openings, and gaps behind dryer vent covers.
**Status:** VERIFIED — confirmed by multiple pest control industry sources (2025-2026).

### Pillar 2: Right Seal + Right Trap Protocol (Verified)
**Claim:** Sealing gaps with the correct material for each location (steel wool + caulk for small gaps, foam sealant for gaps around pipes, hardware cloth for larger openings) prevents re-entry. Combined with a snap trap protocol that actually works, this two-pronged approach ends the cycle.
**Evidence:** Catchmaster (2025) and EcoGuard recommend the steel wool + caulk combo as standard practice. PCI Pest Control notes that spray foam alone is insufficient because mice can chew through it; steel wool provides the resistance.
**Status:** VERIFIED — standard industry protocol across pest control companies.

### Pillar 3: Remove the Reason They Came (Plausible)
**Claim:** Mice enter homes searching for three things: food, warmth, and nesting material. Eliminating these attractants makes your home less appealing even if a gap is temporarily missed.
**Evidence:** Industry standard practice recommended by all pest control companies. Logic is sound, but peer-reviewed residential attribution studies are limited.
**Status:** PLAUSIBLE — universally recommended but limited controlled study data for residential scale.

**Page 4: The Flashlight Tracking Method**
- How to find active entry points vs old ones
- Mouse sign: droppings, urine pillars, rub marks, nesting material
- Timing: inspect at dusk when mice start moving

**Page 5: Sealing Gaps — The Right Material for Each Gap Type**
- Steel wool + caulk: the gold standard for small gaps (under 1/2 inch)
- Expanding foam: for gaps around pipes and larger voids (must be covered with hardware cloth if inside)
- Hardware cloth: for gaps 1/2 inch and larger
- Door sweeps and vent covers: for door and vent gaps
- What NOT to use: spray foam alone, cotton balls, dryer sheets (do not work)

**Page 6: Trap Placement That Actually Catches Mice**
- Snap trap vs glue trap vs live trap: which works
- Where to place traps: along walls, in corners, behind appliances, near active droppings
- The peanut butter + wax paper method: holds bait longer, attracts mice faster
- Common trap mistakes: wrong placement, too few traps, not checking often enough

**Page 7: The Complete 14-Day Protocol**
- Day 1-2: Full entry point inspection + sealing
- Day 3-5: Set traps along walls and near sealed entry points
- Day 6-10: Check daily, re-bait as needed, look for new signs
- Day 11-14: Confirm no new activity, remove remaining traps or transition to monitoring

**Page 8: Why They Came — Removing Food and Nesting Access**
- Food: store pet food in glass/metal containers, clean under appliances, secure pantry items
- Water: fix leaky pipes, dry up moisture in basement/crawl space
- Nesting: clear clutter in garage and basement, store cardboard elsewhere (mice nest in it)

**Page 9: Seasonal Maintenance — Keeping Them Out Year After Year**
- Spring: inspect after winter for foundation shifts and new gaps
- Fall: seal before mice seek warmth indoors
- Winter: monitoring — check traps monthly even without activity
