## Problem Diagnosis

A cat survives a urinary blockage — the emergency vet clears it, catheter removed, sent home. Then what? The owner is handed a bag of medications, a prescription food recommendation, and a vague warning to "watch for signs." What follows is weeks of anxiety: Is this normal? Is he straining or just not done? Why is he licking everything? Is the blood in the urine normal now?

The real problem is **information asymmetry**: veterinarians are busy, cat owners have no baseline for what's normal vs. emergency, and the internet is full of contradictory anecdotes. The recurrence rate is 28–58% (MSPCA-Angell). A blocked cat is statistically likely to block again — but most owners have no structured plan for preventing it.

**Target reader:** Cat owner whose cat just came home from emergency vet treatment for urinary blockage. They are scared, overwhelmed, and want to do everything right.

## 3 Solution Pillars

### Pillar 1: Post-Emergency Recovery (Weeks 1–2)
Focus: Managing the fragile recovery window, medication admin, litter box setup, distinguishing normal healing from warning signs.

**Key facts (VERIFIED):**
- Early signs of recurrence: increased vocalization, frequent litter box trips, licking hind end, straining unproductively (MSPCA-Angell)
- Male cats at higher risk due to longer, narrower urethra (MSPCA-Angell)
- Overweight cats and dry-food-only cats at elevated risk (MSPCA-Angell)
- 24+ hours without urinating is life-threatening — act immediately (MSPCA-Angell)

**Content to cover:**
- Normal post-catheter symptoms vs. warning signs
- Medication schedule tracker template
- Litter box setup: number of boxes, type of litter, location
- Hydration strategies (water fountains, wet food transition)
- Activity restrictions during healing

### Pillar 2: Long-Term Prevention (Month 1 onward)
Focus: Structural changes to reduce recurrence risk. Diet, water intake, environment.

**Key facts (VERIFIED):**
- Prescription urinary diet and increased water intake can reduce recurrence (The Gentle Vet NJ, MSPCA-Angell)
- Struvite and calcium oxalate crystals form from oversaturated urine — dietary management helps (Fairfax Vet)
- Stress is a known trigger for feline lower urinary tract disease (FLUTD) recurrence

**Content to cover:**
- Prescription food guide: what Royal Canin Urinary SO and similar do, how to transition, alternatives
- Water intake optimization: fountains, broth additions, multiple water stations
- Environmental enrichment: reducing stress triggers, multi-cat household management
- Weight management if applicable

### Pillar 3: Emergency Preparedness Kit
Focus: Knowing when to act, what to have ready, how to communicate with vet.

**Content to cover:**
- Warning signs requiring immediate vet visit vs. wait-and-watch
- Emergency vet contact card template (pre-filled with your vet's number + nearest 24hr ER)
- At-home urine monitoring: how to check if cat is producing normal amounts
- Questions to ask at follow-up vet appointment
- Financial prep: pet insurance vs. savings options for future emergencies
